# English AI-slop patterns

Treat these as contextual signals, not banned tokens. Prefer clusters of
independent signals over one punctuation mark or fashionable word.

## Content and framing

### Inflated significance

Watch for ordinary facts framed as milestones, testaments, turning points,
broader movements, enduring legacies, or symbols of an evolving landscape.
Return to the source-backed consequence. Do not invent a concrete consequence
to replace an empty one.

### Promotional copy without evidence

Watch for generic superlatives, frictionless/seamless claims, breathless
adjectives, and reader-addressed sales language in neutral prose. Preserve real
product claims and attributed opinions; lower only unsupported performance.

### Vague authority and notability lists

“Experts say,” “research shows,” media-name lists, and social-proof claims need
their original epistemic status. Never fabricate a source, quotation, year, or
study to make a vague sentence sound specific.

### Superficial participial analysis

Trailing `-ing` clauses often append significance without adding a testable
relationship: *highlighting, underscoring, ensuring, showcasing, reflecting*.
Remove or integrate them when they merely restate the sentence. Preserve
literal technical uses such as traversing a graph or routing a request.

Changing the grammar does not validate the relationship. Never turn a
formulaic trailing clause into a stronger finite assertion such as “ensured,”
“proved,” or “demonstrated.” If the claimed outcome, evidence, or commitment is
not established elsewhere in the source, remove that clause instead of
promoting it.

### Generic setup and conclusion

Cut throat-clearing, roadmap announcements, recap paragraphs, and conclusions
that only restate the introduction. Keep summaries that serve navigation,
compliance, decision-making, or a reader who will scan rather than read linearly.

## Sentence and paragraph shape

### Formulaic contrast

Repeated *not X but Y*, *it is not merely X; it is Y*, negative countdowns, and
false surprise create synthetic emphasis. State the supported point directly
unless the contrast carries real logic.

### Rule-of-three machinery

Three-item lists are normal. The signal is repeated, ornamental triads chosen
for cadence rather than content. Let the source determine whether there are one,
two, three, or more items.

### Manufactured punch

Watch for isolated fragments, one-line dramatic reveals, pull-quote aphorisms,
and metronomic alternation between setup and punchline. Rejoin thoughts when the
fragment exists only to perform confidence. Preserve fragments that belong to
the author or genre.

### Uniform rhythm and modular paragraphs

AI prose often repeats sentence length, transition type, paragraph size, and
topic-summary structure. Restore the actual argumentative dependency between
paragraphs. Do not vary rhythm randomly or turn coherent prose into fragments.

### Fragmented or generic headings

Too many headings, title-case micro-sections, and labels such as *Overview*,
*Challenges*, or *The Future* can expose template scaffolding. Keep headings
that support navigation or a real document schema.

### Diff-anchored prose

Documentation that repeatedly says what was “added,” “updated,” or “replaced”
may describe the edit rather than the current system. State the current behavior
unless the genre is a changelog, migration guide, or release note.

## Diction and syntax

### Statistical-default vocabulary

Words such as *delve, tapestry, landscape, nuanced, robust, seamless, pivotal,
foster, leverage, navigate,* and *underscore* are weak signals. Keep literal or
domain-standard uses, including financial leverage, navigation, and graph
traversal. Rewrite only when the word participates in generic framing.

### Copula avoidance and inflated verbs

Prefer *is, has,* and *does* when the source is simply identifying or possessing
something. Do not replace precise verbs with copulas, and do not flatten legal
or scientific distinctions.

### Passive and subjectless prose

Restore an actor when the source identifies one and active voice is clearer.
Keep passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, intentionally
withheld, or conventional in methods, legal, incident, or reference writing.

### Synonym cycling and repetition anxiety

Repeating the correct term is often clearer than cycling through near-synonyms.
Preserve defined terms and domain vocabulary. Remove accidental repetition only
when it adds no emphasis or cohesion.

### False ranges and false completeness

Constructions such as “from X to Y” or “whether A, B, or C” may imply a spectrum
or exhaustive set that the source does not establish. Use a list or narrower
claim when that is what the evidence supports.

### Hyphens, curly quotes, bold, emoji, and dashes

No punctuation or formatting choice is proof of AI authorship. Fix density,
placement, and genre mismatch. Do not ban em/en dashes globally; preserve a
reasonable author habit, title connector, quoted form, or meaningful aside.

## Interaction residue

Remove chatbot preambles, offers to continue, sycophantic praise, simulated
questions, knowledge-cutoff disclaimers, and generic “hope this helps” endings
when they leaked into the artifact. Keep genuine correspondence where those
sentences perform a social function.

## Positive edit target

- Lead with the source’s actor, fact, question, or decision.
- Prefer source-backed specifics to generic evaluation.
- Let paragraph order carry an argument rather than a reusable list of points.
- Preserve defensible quirks, asides, uncertainty, era-bound language, and
  unevenness already present in the author’s voice.
- Keep technical, legal, academic, and reference prose neutral when neutrality
  is the correct register.
