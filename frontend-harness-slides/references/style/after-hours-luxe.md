# After-Hours Luxe

## Aesthetic Thesis
The screen behaves like a private room in a late-night hotel bar: dim, warm,
magnetic — you lean in. A single oversized headline glows like neon through
smoke, pearl-cream panels of light float against the dark. The three-second
impression is intimate couture editorial, not corporate keynote.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Sultry, confident, magnetic — the register of a glossy fashion spread or a
midnight perfume campaign. Reach for it on brand reveals, premium product
launches, couture positioning, and any deck that needs exclusivity over
information. It fights daytime utility, dense data, and anything corporate; the
intimacy that reads as luxury here would read as self-indulgent in a boardroom
briefing.

## Color Temperament
Live in a nocturnal value world: a deep warm-black ground — never pure flat
black, always carrying a faint warm undertone, as if lit from one corner by a
studio softbox — punctuated by pearl-cream paper insets that read as held
light. One single hot, expressive accent (the feeling of hot-pink or magenta
ink) is the entire chromatic system; it appears in the hero headline, in
eyebrow kickers, in inline emphasis, and in a soft halo behind the largest
type. It is scarce and therefore electric. Refuse a second chromatic accent,
pure white, and filled background patterns. Why: the whole mood rests on one
spark in a dark room; introduce a second color and the intimacy collapses into a
palette.

## Typographic Voice
Pair an elegant high-contrast editorial serif for every display and headline
moment with an ultra-light geometric sans for body prose and a mono uppercase
voice for labels, page numbers, and metadata. The serif carries the couture
personality — it runs enormous, sometimes half the canvas, and glows with a
soft halo. The sans whispers context at a fraction of the size; the mono
uppercase, widely tracked, is the edition stamp. Hierarchy comes almost
entirely through scale and the serif-vs-sans split, not through weight. Why:
the drama of a single enormous serif voice dropping to near-silence is what
makes a headline feel like a magazine spread rather than a slide title; an even
hierarchy kills the editorial tension.

## Spatial Temperament
Asymmetric and sparse-to-medium density. One hero moment — a giant script or a
pearl-cream inset panel — anchors the composition, and two to four small
editorial fragments orbit the remaining margin with deliberate imbalance. The
invariant is the ratio of dark ground to lit surface: the black must dominate,
and the pearl-cream and accent elements must feel like islands of light within
it, not a grid of panels. Avoid centered symmetry; favor off-center drift and
hanging indents.

## Material & Atmosphere
Depth is atmospheric, never structural. The ground carries a faint radial warm
lift from one corner, simulating a softbox on photographic paper. A near-
invisible film-grain overlay adds photographic texture that keeps the surface
from reading as digital flatness. A hairline interior frame of pearl-cream at
very low opacity edges every slide like the gutter of a magazine page. No drop
shadows, card elevations, or visible interface chrome. The atmosphere is still,
close, and slightly dangerous.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks are: a soft luminous halo behind the largest serif
headlines (neon-saturated ink on photographic paper); a mono uppercase eyebrow
kicker in the accent color above every primary headline; a pearl-cream left-
rule callout rail beside charts or chapter explanations; and the interior
hairline frame on every slide. The accent kicker above a serif headline is the
system's most reliable rhythm; omit it and the slide loses its couture
signature.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is smooth, confident, and slow — the tempo of someone who knows
everyone is already watching. Favor long eased entrances, gentle drifts, and
soft fade-to-rest transitions over anything snappy or mechanical. The hero
element settles into place; it does not bounce. Interaction, where present, is
quiet and assured. Why: the mood is magnetic composure; fast or bouncy motion
contradicts the late-night stillness the whole system is built on.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms

Identity Invariants:
- Deep warm-black ground with a faint warm radial lift from one corner
- Near-invisible film-grain overlay and hairline pearl-cream interior frame
- One single hot expressive accent color — never two
- Pearl-cream paper insets as the light-surface language (not white cards)
- Elegant high-contrast editorial serif as the sole display voice
- Ultra-light sans body; mono uppercase tracked labels and metadata
- Accent-colored kicker eyebrow above every primary headline
- Asymmetric composition; no centered symmetry

Freedoms:
- Which element is the hero (script headline, inset panel, stat, quote)
- Exact scale of the display serif — ranges freely from intimate to enormous
- Placement of the hero and orbit of supporting fragments
- Specific accent hue within the hot-magenta family
- Whether a pearl-cream inset reads as a full panel, callout rail, or tile
- Transition and entrance choices, so long as tempo stays slow and confident

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when it gets corporate or loud: a second accent color, pure white
backgrounds, filled texture or noise patterns, multiple competing hero
elements, centered geometric symmetry, fast bouncy motion, visible drop shadows
or card elevations, or body copy set in the display serif. If the content needs
daytime clarity, dense data, operational comparison, or premium restraint
without the nocturnal mood, switch to Minimal Product Keynote instead.
