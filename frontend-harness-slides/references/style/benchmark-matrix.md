# Benchmark Matrix

## Aesthetic Thesis
A clean, analytical evaluation matrix that moves judgment away from surface
metrics and toward real, structural capability, presented as legible comparative
evidence. The point is a fair, readable comparison you can trust.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Analytical, comparative, and even-handed — the register of a rigorous evaluation.
Reach for it on tool comparisons, evaluation criteria, and data-heavy benchmarks,
where structured comparison is the message. As a reading-first style it carries
real detail, but it fights narrative keynote scenes; a matrix has nothing to do
when there is nothing to compare.

## Color Temperament
Keep a bright, neutral ground, optionally with quiet alternating row tints for
scanability, and reserve a clear positive tone and a clear negative tone to mark
results. Color encodes outcome — met versus missing — not decoration. Why: the
fairness and readability of a comparison depend on a neutral surface where
positive/negative marks stand out; a colorful ground would bias or blur the
reading.

## Typographic Voice
Favor a clean, highly legible, neutral character sized for comfortable scanning of
rows, columns, and figures. Keep numbers and labels crisp and aligned. Why: the
whole value is easy, fair comparison, so legibility beats personality; an
expressive voice would slow the read and imply bias.

## Spatial Temperament
It wants a comparison sensibility: criteria set against options so the reader can
scan across and down, or a grid of standout metric cards. Whether it is a full
comparison table or a metric grid is free; the invariant is a legible structure
that lets the eye compare like against like without hunting.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface is clean and analytical: bright, flat, lightly ruled or row-tinted,
free of texture. Depth is minimal — structure comes from the grid, alignment, and
result marks, not shadow. The atmosphere is objective, orderly, and calm.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks are comparison vocabulary: a criteria axis, option
columns, clear positive/negative result marks, and standout figures. Use them to
carry real evaluation — real criteria, real results. The scannable comparison
grid is the signature; a decorative layout that hides the comparison breaks it.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is comparison emphasis: rows or columns highlight, a winning cell or a
key figure is promoted, results reveal in order. Interaction can sort, filter, or
expand a criterion. Keep motion clarifying, not decorative. Why: the animation
should aid comparison, so motion that does not sharpen the read wastes the matrix.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Keep constant to stay this style: a bright neutral ground, clear positive and
negative result tones, a clean legible scanning voice, a like-against-like
comparison structure, minimal analytical depth, and clarifying motion. Free to
vary on every slide: table versus metric grid, which criteria and options appear,
the exact result tones, and how comparisons are emphasized.

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when comparison gets hard: a dark or busy background, decorative color
that competes with result marks, an expressive typeface, a layout that hides the
like-for-like reading, or motion unrelated to comparison. If there is nothing to
compare or the moment is narrative, the matrix is empty — use a keynote or memo
style instead. If the content calls for strict mathematical grid discipline with visible column rules rather than a flexible comparison matrix, switch to Objective Swiss Grid. If it is a linear readiness or acceptance check rather than multi-dimensional comparison, move to Checklist Ledger.
