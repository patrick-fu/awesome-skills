# Blackboard Chalk Talk

## Aesthetic Thesis
A live board where someone is thinking through an idea in front of you — drawing
the diagram, working the formula, connecting concepts by hand. It feels handmade,
educational, and reasoning-first, as if the logic is being derived in the moment
rather than presented finished.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Patient, explanatory, and trustworthy — the register of a good teacher at the
board. Reach for it on core principles, conceptual formulas, derivations, and any
"let me show you why this works" moment where visible reasoning beats a polished
claim. It fights glossy corporate polish and hype; the chalk honesty reads as
sincere in teaching contexts and out of place in a slick sales pitch.

## Color Temperament
Set a deep, matte board tone — a dark green or dark slate — that reads as a real
chalkboard and lets light marks glow softly against it. Marks are chalky and
slightly imperfect: a soft off-white for most writing, with a small set of pastel
chalk accents (a gentle yellow, a soft mint) to highlight or group. Keep colors
few and chalk-soft. Why: the low, matte darkness plus soft chalk marks is what
sells handwritten reasoning; pure black or bright saturated color turns the board
into a screen.

## Typographic Voice
Favor a light, handwritten character for the human writing and annotations,
paired with a clean, light technical voice for formulas and precise terms — the
mix of "written by hand" and "notation" is exactly how a real chalk talk looks.
Keep the hand thin and chalky rather than heavy. Why: a printed, heavy, or
ornamental voice breaks the illusion that this is being drawn live.

## Spatial Temperament
It wants the loose, evolving layout of a working board: a central idea being built
up, with hand-drawn arrows, brackets, and margin notes relating parts as the
reasoning unfolds. It can breathe (a single hero formula) or fill up (a worked
derivation), but it should always feel drawn in sequence rather than
pre-composed. What sits where, how arrows route, and where annotations land are
all free; the invariant is the hand-derived, reasoning-in-progress temperament.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface is a matte board with faint chalk dust, light streaks, and the soft
imperfection of a real writing surface. Depth is nearly flat — everything lives
on the board plane — with presence coming from chalk texture and the softness of
marks, not from shadows or gloss. The atmosphere is quiet, focused, and studious.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks are chalk artifacts: hand-drawn diagrams and arrows,
underlines and boxes for emphasis, worked formulas, and margin notes in a lighter
hand. Use them to carry the reasoning — an arrow shows a dependency, a box marks
the result. The slight roughness of chalk is the signature; crisp vector-perfect
lines break it.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is drawing in real time: lines and diagrams sketch themselves stroke by
stroke, formulas appear term by term, arrows extend to connect ideas as the
argument advances. Interaction can step through a derivation or reveal the next
line of reasoning. Keep the tempo like a hand writing — steady, sequential,
slightly uneven. Why: the motion is the act of teaching, so instant reveals or
slick transitions remove the worked-out-live quality that makes the style teach.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Keep constant to stay this style: a matte dark-board base, soft chalky off-white
marks with a few pastel chalk accents, a light handwritten hand paired with a
clean notation voice, a near-flat board plane, hand-drawn reasoning artifacts,
and stroke-by-stroke drawing motion. Free to vary on every slide: what is being
derived or diagrammed, the arrangement of marks, the specific chalk accents, and
how the reasoning is stepped through.

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when it gets polished: crisp vector lines, flat solid fills, bright
saturated color, a heavy printed typeface, glossy depth, or everything appearing
at once instead of being worked out. If the content is a finished corporate
result rather than a line of reasoning, do not chalk it up — the handmade honesty
feels like a costume; use a cleaner report or keynote style. If the content needs
formal calm evidence and document authority rather than live board reasoning,
switch to Research Memo. If it wants warmer workshop collaboration with sticky
notes and emoji instead of chalk formulas, move to Sketch Board Emoji.
