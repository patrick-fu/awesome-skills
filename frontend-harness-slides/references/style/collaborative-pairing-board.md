# Collaborative Pairing Board

## Aesthetic Thesis
A clean, digital whiteboard where two distinct partners work side by side — human
and system, or two roles — with a clear division of labor and visible points
where they sync. The point is cooperation with boundaries.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Clean, cooperative, and professional — the register of a well-run pairing
session. Reach for it on role division, pair workflows, hand-in-hand processes,
and synchronization stories, where showing who does what and where they align is
the message. It works live and reads afterward, but it fights highly expressive
or emotional talks; without two parties to coordinate, the board is half empty.

## Color Temperament
Keep a clean, light, neutral ground so the two partners and their boundary read
clearly, with a calm professional accent to mark alignment or the active role.
Restraint is the mood — this is a workspace, not a spectacle. Why: the clarity of
"two sides, one boundary, shared sync points" depends on a quiet ground; loud
color would blur the division the style exists to show.

## Typographic Voice
Favor a clean, modern, neutral character for structured labels and lists, with a
subtle technical or monospaced voice for role tags or step markers. Keep both
partners' text in the same family so neither side feels like a different system.
Why: the cooperation reads best when the type is calm and even-handed; an
expressive voice would tilt attention to one side.

## Spatial Temperament
It wants a legible sense of two parties and a shared seam between them — a
division that makes "this partner, that partner, and where they meet" obvious,
plus a sense of sequence or sync. Whether the division is a split, a timeline of
alternating turns, or another arrangement is free; the invariant is a clear
two-party structure with visible alignment points.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface feels like a clean digital board: light, flat, uncluttered, with
gentle dividers and soft cards rather than heavy chrome. Depth is minimal and
tidy — light elevation, clear separation. The atmosphere is calm, organized, and
cooperative.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks are collaboration cues: two clear role identities
(an avatar or icon per side), a boundary or divider between them, and explicit
sync points or checkpoints where they align. Use them to make roles and handoffs
unmistakable. The visible boundary-plus-sync is the signature; a single
undivided column breaks it.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is alternating and cooperative: turns reveal side to side, a sync point
lights when both align, a handoff passes attention across the boundary.
Interaction can step through the collaboration or expand a role's detail. Keep
motion balanced between the two sides. Why: the animation is showing coordination,
so motion that favors or ignores one side would misrepresent the pairing.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Keep constant to stay this style: a clean light neutral ground with a calm
professional accent, an even-handed neutral type voice, a clear two-party
structure with visible sync points, tidy low-chrome depth, and balanced
alternating motion. Free to vary on every slide: split versus timeline versus
other two-party arrangement, the role identities, the accent, and how turns and
syncs are revealed.

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when the pairing disappears: a busy or dark dramatic background, loud
color that favors one side, an expressive display typeface, a single undivided
layout, or no visible sync. If the content is one voice or one big claim, there is
no pairing to show — use a keynote or report style instead.
