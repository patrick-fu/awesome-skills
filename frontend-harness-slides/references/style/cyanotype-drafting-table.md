# Cyanotype Drafting Table

## Aesthetic Thesis
The screen behaves like a sheet of blueprint paper laid flat on a drafting
table under a north-facing window: the blue is the paper itself, not a screen
painted blue, and every line reads as a measured decision rather than a
decorative gesture. In three seconds you feel precision, calm, and craft —
this was drawn, not generated.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Serene, meticulous, and quietly confident — the mood of an engineer's bench
rather than a control room. Reach for it on architectural diagrams, system
schematics, data-blueprint explainers, and technical narratives where the
audience needs to follow structure and proportion. It fights dark-neon
urgency, consumer gloss, and emotional spectacle; the measured stillness that
reads as authoritative here would feel inert in a launch or alert context.

## Color Temperament
Live entirely inside a deep Prussian-blue ground — the blue is the substrate,
the paper, the air. Linework lives in two registers: chalk-white for the
primary drawing and a paler, desaturated cyan for secondary construction
lines, dimension guides, and faint grid. A single warm annotation accent
(drafting red or amber in feeling) is permitted and kept extremely scarce —
it touches only a deliberate callout or a single highlighted path, never body
text or large-area fill. Why: monochrome discipline makes this a serious
artifact rather than a themed UI; one warm spark against the cool field
reads as a human hand, and a second accent collapses it into decoration.
Refuse bright cyans, saturated purples, and any value that glows rather
than sits.

## Typographic Voice
Type here is legend, not headline. Labels carry a drafting-stencil character:
monospaced or mono-adjacent, uppercase for dimension callouts and coordinate
markers, set small and precise. Supporting metadata — title-block fields,
scale indicators, revision notes — sits in a tidy, compact voice that reads
as furniture rather than rhetoric. There is no "hero headline"; the diagram
is the hero, and type serves it by being legible, uniform, and unemotional.
Why: when type refuses to perform, the drawing gains all the authority; an
expressive display voice would fight the blueprint's instrumentality.

## Spatial Temperament
The page is a drafting surface, not a composition. Density is moderate to
high — dimension lines, labels, and annotation marks fill the field — but
every element has a reason and a measured position. The grid is a faint
construction aid, never a visual feature. The invariant is proportional
discipline: elements relate to each other by alignment and offset, not by
centering or visual balance. Hero diagram and metadata placement is free;
what must not happen is decorative spacing or elements placed for "balance"
rather than legibility of the drawing.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface is cyanotype paper: deep, matte, slightly fibrous in feeling —
you sense the paper before you sense the screen. Depth comes from line
weight and value contrast, not from shadows or elevation. The atmosphere is
still, workshop-quiet, and unhurried; nothing pulses, glows, or competes.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks are: a tidy title-block in one corner carrying
metadata in small uniform type; dimension lines with uppercase callouts
framing the hero diagram; pale-cyan construction lines showing the invisible
geometry; and the single warm annotation used as a deliberate handwritten
note. These are the maker's marks of a blueprint — use them to clarify the
drawing, never to fill empty space.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is precise and mechanical, like a drafting arm swinging into place
or a line being drawn with a ruler. Favor clean linear reveals, sequential
dimension callouts appearing one by one, and diagram elements that draw
themselves along their own geometry. Keep tempo steady and unrushed; nothing
bounces, overshoots, or eases with personality. Why: motion must agree
with the blueprint's measured discipline; organic or playful easing
contradicts the workshop calm.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Identity Invariants:
- Deep Prussian-blue ground that reads as paper, not screen
- Chalk-white primary linework plus pale-cyan construction lines
- One scarce warm annotation accent, never more
- Drafting-stencil label voice with uppercase dimension callouts
- Title-block metadata furniture in a corner
- The hero is an orthographic or isometric diagram, not a headline
- Measured, geometry-aligned motion

Freedoms:
- Which diagram or system is the hero
- Placement of the title-block and which metadata it carries
- Exact density of dimension lines and construction guides
- Where the single warm annotation lands
- Tempo of the reveal sequence and which elements animate

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when the blueprint becomes a theme: grids used as decoration rather
than construction, dark-neon glow or saturated accents, multiple warm colors
competing, type that tries to be a headline instead of a legend, soft
organic motion, or any consumer-gloss surface treatment. For routed technical flows, switch to Signal Pipeline instead. If it requires strict analytical grid authority with mathematical column discipline instead of drafting-table craft, move to Objective Swiss Grid.
