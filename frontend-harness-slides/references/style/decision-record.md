# Decision Record

## Aesthetic Thesis
A formal, lightweight architectural decision record that makes the reasoning
behind a choice explicit: context, decision, trade-offs, and how it will be
verified. The point is rigor — the page proves a choice was made deliberately.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Rigorous, architectural, and deliberate — the register of official technical
documentation. Reach for it on technical decisions, trade-offs, and architectural
boundaries, where explaining the "why" and the costs matters as much as the
choice. As a reading-first style it holds real detail, but it fights playful
workshops and emotional talks; the formal rigor reassures in an engineering review
and deadens a lively room.

## Color Temperament
Keep a structured, light, cool ground with thin, sharp rules, and reserve a
formal accent — a serious blue or a considered green — for an approval or status
mark. Color is procedural, not expressive. Why: the authority of a record comes
from a disciplined, document-like surface; extra color would make a rigorous
decision look casual.

## Typographic Voice
Favor a clean, structured, technical character — often monospaced — to evoke
official documentation, with clear labeled sections. Keep it exact and unadorned.
Why: the credibility of a decision record rests on looking like a real,
disciplined document; a soft or expressive voice would undercut the rigor.

## Spatial Temperament
It wants a document structure of labeled reasoning blocks — context, decision,
trade-offs, verification — or a clear trade-off comparison, so a reader can follow
the argument to its conclusion. Whether it is a single record sheet or a
side-by-side trade-off matrix is free; the invariant is explicit, labeled
reasoning that leads to a stated, verifiable decision.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface feels like a formal spec sheet: light, flat, sharply ruled, precise.
Depth is nearly nil — structure comes from labeled blocks and rules, not shadow.
The atmosphere is exact, serious, and considered.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks are ADR vocabulary: a record header with an approval
or status mark, clearly labeled reasoning blocks, and explicit trade-off framing.
Use them to carry the real decision — real context, real costs, real verification.
The labeled-reasoning-to-decision grammar is the signature; unstructured prose
breaks it.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is decision progression: reasoning blocks reveal in order, a status
resolves to approved, a trade-off's two sides settle for comparison. Interaction
can expand a block or reveal a cost. Keep motion restrained and orderly. Why: the
page is a formal record, so lively motion would contradict its deliberateness.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Keep constant to stay this style: a structured light cool ground with sharp
rules, a formal procedural accent, a clean technical/mono documentation voice,
explicit labeled reasoning leading to a verifiable decision, near-flat spec-sheet
depth, and restrained orderly motion. Free to vary on every slide: record sheet
versus trade-off matrix, which reasoning blocks appear, the accent, and how the
argument is revealed.

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when it loses rigor: a warm or dark dramatic background, decorative
color, an expressive typeface, unstructured prose with no labeled reasoning, or
showy motion. If the setting is a playful workshop or an emotional talk, the
formal record deadens it — use a warmer or more expressive style instead.
