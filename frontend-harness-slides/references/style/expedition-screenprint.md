# Expedition Screenprint

## Aesthetic Thesis
The screen behaves like a WPA poster pulled fresh off the silkscreen: flat
planes of warm ink, visible grain, a horizon you can feel the altitude of.
In three seconds you register a place, a promise, and a quiet civic
invitation — not a sales pitch but a poster you would stop to read on a
lamppost at dusk.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Civic-optimistic, earnest, outdoorsy — the mood of public works and shared
landscape. Reach for it on product launches with a public-good angle,
geographic or exploratory themes, community initiatives, and any narrative
that benefits from a sense of place. It fights cynical luxury, clinical
data density, and somber tones; the honesty that reads as hopeful here
would read as naive in a boardroom readout.

## Color Temperament
Live in a limited set of warm spot inks laid as flat, unmodulated planes:
dusk orange, forest depth, sky-teal, and sand. No gradients pretending to be
light — each color is a declaration, not a simulation. One accent may step
forward for emphasis but stays within the same flat-ink family; nothing
glows, nothing shines. Refuse photographic color, pure process white, and
any value spread wider than the poster can hold. Why: the civic warmth
comes from the *limit* — a small, chosen palette feels like conviction;
expand it and you get decoration instead of declaration.

## Typographic Voice
Display type is a confident condensed sans set in capitals at poster scale —
short, declarative headlines that land like stenciled signs. It does not
hover over the image; it sits *inside* the illustrated plane, sharing the
same flat ground as the landscape. Supporting text stays small, plain, and
unpretentious — the voice of a park ranger's handbill, not a museum plaque.
Hierarchy is blunt: the caps shout the place name, the body whispers the
details. Why: the caps carry the civic optimism — they feel public,
outdoors, unapologetic; shrinking them or softening them into title case
turns a poster into a brochure.

## Spatial Temperament
Density is moderate and intentional — a landscape, a headline, maybe one
supporting block, never more than the eye can take in as one composition.
Focal logic is horizon-based: a stylized ground line anchors the lower
half, the headline claims sky or mid-ground. The grid feels loose and
poster-like, not rigid. The invariant is the relationship between flat ink
plane and type-as-object — they share the same surface, not stacked
layers. Exact placement is free to follow the story.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface is paper with a tooth — ink grain, slight misfit between color
planes, the honest imperfection of a screen pull. Depth comes from
overlapping flat shapes and stylized landscape distance, never from shadows
or light simulation. The atmosphere is open-air and public-spirited; it
smells like a national park brochure and a town hall announcement at once.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks: a stylized horizon or altitude line giving
every slide a sense of place; flat ink planes with visible grain that never
pretend to be anything but ink; condensed capitals sitting within the
landscape rather than above it; and a small plain attribution behaving like
a printer's mark — present, functional, unadorned. Use them to anchor each
slide to the same world, not as decoration applied after the fact.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is poster-static or gently drifting — a slow pan across a stylized
landscape, a soft fade between flat planes, the quiet settling of a headline
into its ink bed. Nothing bounces, nothing snaps, nothing draws attention
to itself. Interaction, if present, is deliberate and infrequent — stepping
through vistas, revealing a detail within the flat plane. Why: the style's
power is in its stillness and conviction; energetic motion contradicts the
civic composure and makes the poster feel like a television ad.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Identity Invariants:
- Flat limited spot inks as unmodulated planes — no gradients, no light sim
- Visible screenprint grain or ink-misfit texture across color surfaces
- Condensed sans capitals at poster scale carrying the headline voice
- Type integrated within the illustrated plane, not floating above it
- Stylized landscape or horizon as a compositional anchor
- Civic-optimistic emotional register — earnest, public, outdoors

Freedoms:
- Which spot hues populate the palette (warm-earth-and-sky family)
- Exact horizon placement and landscape form (ridge, coast, valley, plateau)
- Headline length and content, as long as it stays declarative
- Supporting text density and layout
- Whether motion is static-poster or gentle-drift
- Any interaction model, if deliberate and restrained

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when the flat-ink conviction is betrayed: photographic realism or
lens effects, gradient meshes pretending to be sky, luxury minimalism that
strips out the grain and warmth, somber dark palettes, type floating in UI
chrome above the image, or playful bouncy motion that turns the poster into
a cartoon. If the content calls for a cultural institution's gravitas — a
museum, a biennale, an art-world context — switch to Solar Biennale Poster
instead of trying to make this screenprint feel like a gallery wall.
