# Field Notes Report

## Aesthetic Thesis
An explorer's or researcher's field notebook — tactile, practical, and
observation-led — that ties digital workflows to physical settings and real human
habits. The point is grounded observation: what was actually seen, recorded by
hand.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Observational, tactile, and honest — the register of notes taken in the field.
Reach for it on user-research observations, physical setup notes, and tactile case
studies, where connecting software to real behavior makes it credible. As a
reading-first style it carries real detail, but it fights formal legal or finance
decks; the handwritten warmth reads as genuine in research and as unserious in a
compliance setting.

## Color Temperament
Build on a soft, aged-paper ground — a warm cream or notebook yellow — with dark
sepia or charcoal writing and a warm amber or terracotta accent for emphasis.
Keep it warm, faded, and analog. Why: the aged-paper warmth is what makes
observations feel real and hand-recorded; cool or bright color would turn a
notebook into a UI and lose the credibility of "I was there."

## Typographic Voice
Pair a warm, legible handwriting or informal character for titles and margin
notes with a clean, readable voice for the observations themselves, so the page
reads as real notes with structured findings. Keep the hand legible, not messy.
Why: the mix of "recorded by a person" and "clearly readable" is what sells honest
field observation; all-handwriting turns unreadable, all-clean loses the notebook.

## Spatial Temperament
It wants a notebook sensibility: a logged header, observations listed with
hand-marked bullets, and room for a simple sketch of a real setup beside the
findings. Whether it is a field log or a sketch-plus-observations arrangement is
free; the invariant is hand-recorded observation that reads as grounded and real.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface feels like real paper: soft grain, aged tone, the slight imperfection
of a used notebook. Depth is nearly flat and papery, with warmth from texture
rather than shadow. The atmosphere is quiet, curious, and grounded.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks are field-notebook vocabulary: a log header,
hand-drawn bullets and margin notes, simple observational sketches, and warm
annotations. Use them to carry real observations — real settings, real behaviors.
The hand-recorded observation feel is the signature; a slick typeset layout breaks
it.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is note-taking: observations reveal one by one as if being written, a
sketch draws in, a margin note appears. Interaction can reveal a note's detail or
step through observations. Keep motion gentle and hand-paced. Why: the animation
echoes recording notes, so slick mechanical motion would break the analog,
observed feel.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Keep constant to stay this style: a soft aged-paper ground, sepia/charcoal writing
with a warm accent, a legible handwriting voice paired with a readable observation
voice, hand-recorded observational structure, flat papery depth, and gentle
hand-paced motion. Free to vary on every slide: field log versus sketch layout,
which observations appear, the warm accent, and how notes are revealed.

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when it goes slick: a bright white or dark technical background, cool or
neon color, a purely typeset look with no hand marks, a rigid corporate layout, or
mechanical motion. If the deck is formal legal, finance, or high-gravitas, the
notebook warmth undercuts it — use a memo or decision style instead. If the content wants poster-style civic optimism with flat ink conviction and landscape horizon rather than notebook observation, switch to Expedition Screenprint. If it needs looser workshop energy with hand-drawn marks and emoji actors instead of grounded field notes, move to Sketch Board Emoji.
