# Kitchen Prep Station

## Aesthetic Thesis
A warm professional kitchen prep station where raw, messy input is treated as raw
ingredients and the work of cleaning it up is chopping, trimming, and plating into
something ready to serve. The point is transformation you can almost taste.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Warm, concrete, and hands-on — the register of a friendly chef showing prep.
Reach for it on raw-to-clean transformations, pre-processing, and step-by-step
refinement, where making the cleanup tangible helps it land. It works live and
reads afterward, but it fights formal technical governance; the cozy kitchen tone
would feel out of place in a rigorous compliance context.

## Color Temperament
Build on a warm, soft, appetizing ground — an orange-tinted cream — with earthy
kitchen accents (a terracotta or cooked-orange, warm wood browns, a fresh herb
green) that feel like real food and surfaces. Keep it warm and natural rather than
neon. Why: the warmth is what makes the transformation feel inviting and human;
cool or clinical color would turn a kitchen into a lab.

## Typographic Voice
Favor a friendly, rounded character or a warm, approachable serif for titles and
step labels, readable and unintimidating. Keep it welcoming rather than technical.
Why: the whole appeal is that a messy job feels doable and pleasant, so a cold or
severe voice would fight the warmth.

## Spatial Temperament
It wants a clear before-and-after or a sequence of prep steps — a sense of raw
going in and clean coming out, or of ordered stages toward a plated result.
Whether it is a prep-board contrast, a run of recipe steps, or another kitchen
arrangement is free; the invariant is a legible transformation from raw to
refined.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface feels like a warm kitchen: soft paper or board texture, cozy light,
tactile ingredients and tools. Depth comes from gentle real-world warmth and soft
shadow, not flat graphics. The atmosphere is inviting, active, and a little homey.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks are prep cues: a cutting-board or workspace surface,
raw input with filler visibly trimmed away, plated clean output, and simple
food-or-utensil markers for steps. Use them to carry the real refinement — what
was removed, what remains, what the finished result is. The tactile raw-to-plated
vocabulary is the signature; abstract cards break it.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is prep in progress: filler gets crossed out or swept away, ingredients
move through steps, a clean result plates up. Interaction can step through the
recipe or reveal a stage. Keep motion warm and hands-on rather than mechanical.
Why: the animation is the act of preparing, so cold or purely technical motion
would lose the tangible, human feel.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Keep constant to stay this style: a warm appetizing cream ground with earthy
kitchen accents, a friendly rounded or warm-serif voice, a legible raw-to-refined
transformation, cozy tactile depth, and warm hands-on motion. Free to vary on
every slide: prep-board contrast versus step sequence, which ingredients and
tools appear, the specific warm accents, and how the transformation is revealed.

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when it goes cold and clinical: a stark white or dark technical
background, neon or corporate color, a severe geometric typeface, abstract cards
with no raw-to-clean story, or sterile mechanical motion. If the content is formal
governance or pure data, the kitchen tone undercuts it — use a report or decision
style instead. If the content wants loose workshop energy with emoji actors and
sticky notes rather than structured kitchen steps, switch to Sketch Board Emoji.
If it needs hands-on observational field-notebook grounding instead of recipe
transformation, move to Field Notes Report.
