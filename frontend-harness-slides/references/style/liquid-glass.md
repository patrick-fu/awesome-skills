# Liquid Glass

## Aesthetic Thesis
The screen behaves like a museum vitrine lowered over a precious object: you see
the artifact clearly through a pane of real glass, and that pane — far from
hiding anything — makes what it holds feel more valuable, more worth leaning in
to examine. In three seconds you sense that the layer between you and the content
is not a barrier but a sign of care.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Refined, luminous, spatially confident — crafted and weighty rather than flat
and decorative. Reach for it on product showcases, layered dashboards, spatial
interfaces, and any slide where showing what lies beneath adds real meaning.
It fights flat single-layer design and the lazy instinct to blur everything;
the moment glass stops revealing something meaningful, it becomes mere
decoration.

## Color Temperament
Glass itself is nearly colorless — a neutral medium picking up ambient
temperature from what lies behind. The world behind carries the real color
story; the glass surface adds only a faint cool bloom. Accent lives in the
light: a specular catch along an edge, a subtle highlight tracing the
boundary. These are rare and precise — one glint
per surface, never a rim-light parade. Why: glass derives its beauty from
what passes through it, not its own tint; color the glass and you kill the
depth it exists to create. Refuse colored glass washes and any tint that
obscures rather than refracts.

## Typographic Voice
Type operates at two depths and must hold its own at both. On the glass
surface, type holds its edge — the material sharpens it slightly,
so it reads with confidence even against a busy field behind. Behind the glass,
type softens: it loses sharpness but keeps its shape, still readable.
The hierarchy gap between on-glass and behind-glass is the spatial story
— it should feel like inches, not points. Why: the whole
premise is that both layers matter, so type must function at two depths
simultaneously; if the behind-glass layer is illegible, the glass has no
reason to exist.

## Spatial Temperament
Glass panels float free in space, never stretched to fill the viewport. Each
surface hugs its content with generous padding, reading as a vessel for
something specific, not wallpaper. Multiple glass surfaces maintain clear
z-order: one clearly in front, one clearly behind, never ambiguous. The
invariant is the relationship — glass sized to content, content visible
behind, depth gaps that read instantly — not any fixed grid. Free to place
panels where the spatial story makes sense.

## Material & Atmosphere
Glass has real physical presence. A thin, bright edge highlight traces the
top curve of each surface, catching light like a real pane. A faint specular
glint sometimes plays across a corner, suggesting subtle curvature.
Refraction is present but gentle — content behind shifts slightly at the
edges, never distorted beyond recognition. Depth comes from layered
translucency, not shadows; the atmosphere is luminous and breathable.

## Signature Gestures
Three maker's marks define the style: the edge highlight signaling
thickness, the softened-legible behind-glass content proving real depth,
and the weighty settle-in of panels rather than flat fades. Each mark
reinforces that glass is a material with properties, not a blur filter.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement obeys physical intuition. Glass surfaces ease in with a slight
settle — a tiny overshoot and calm rest, like a real object finding its
place. Between layered glass panels, parallax is gentle and consistent: the
front pane drifts more than the one behind, reinforcing z-space without
calling attention to itself. Interaction is confirmed by a subtle compression
or focus ring respecting the material, never by a flash or bounce. Why:
because the style asks you to believe glass is real, its motion must agree;
linear fades and snappy springs would expose it as a visual trick.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Identity Invariants:
- Glass always carries edge highlight and subtle refraction — never just a flat blur.
- Glass is used sparingly — on buttons, cards, panels; never as a full-screen wash.
- Real depth hierarchy behind every surface — what lies behind is visible and functional.
- Glass is sized to its content, not stretched to fill.
- Foreground legibility on glass is preserved through vibrancy.

Freedoms:
- Exact shape and corner character of each glass surface.
- Number of glass layers on one slide.
- What content lives on the glass versus behind it.
- Ambient color temperature of the world behind.
- Motion easing and parallax amounts.
- Whether a given slide uses glass at all — some can be entirely flat.

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when glass becomes a default: frosted blur on every surface, no depth
logic, nothing meaningful to reveal. It also dies when material physics are
missing — no edge highlight, no refraction, just a semi-transparent card
reading as a UI widget rather than a pane. Other killers: glass stretched
full-screen like wallpaper, behind-glass content so blurred it's illegible,
too many stacked panes to parse, and any colored tint turning glass into a
colored rectangle. If the content doesn't benefit from layers, drop the
glass and switch to Minimal Product Keynote for premium restraint without
the material overhead.
