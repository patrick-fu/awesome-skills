# Machine-Age Deco

## Aesthetic Thesis
The screen behaves like a grand ocean liner's dining saloon: engineered
symmetry, lacquered depth, and metallic precision, where every line is a
structural beam and every ornament is a rivet. The three-second impression is
monumental and unyielding — a machine-age cathedral built for one idea to stand
at the altar of geometry.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Grand, stately, and engineered — the register of a transatlantic poster or a
great exhibition hall. Reach for it on flagship product launches, brand
manifestos, award ceremonies, and any moment that demands ceremony and scale.
It fights intimate storytelling, casual conversation, and dense data; the
symmetric monumentality that reads as triumph here would read as cold or
overbearing in a human-scale moment.

## Color Temperament
Live in a world of deep lacquer grounds — oxblood, midnight, jade, or absolute
black — set against a single metallic second ink: gold, brass, or chrome, flat
and unblended, never gradient. The palette is tiny and disciplined; the drama
is the chasm between the absorbing dark and the reflective metal. Accent is
structural, not decorative — it traces frames, rules, and geometric devices
rather than filling fields. Why: the metallic second ink reads as engineered
opulence precisely because it is scarce and flat; spread it across large areas
or soften it into a gradient and it collapses into cheap glitter. Refuse pastels,
friendly mid-tones, and any color that whispers rather than declares.

## Typographic Voice
Display type is tall, narrow, and geometric — capitals that stand at attention
with generous tracking, letterforms that feel carved from metal rather than
drawn with a pen. Hierarchy is absolute: the title is a monument, everything
else is a foundation stone. Body copy, when it appears at all, is set in a
quiet, recessive voice that does not compete with the display's authority.
Why: the tall condensed caps carry the machine-age DNA — they read as
engineered and monumental, and their contrast with the recessive body is what
makes the page feel like a poster rather than a paragraph.

## Spatial Temperament
Composition is symmetric and monumental. The page has a central axis, and
elements align to it with the precision of a building's facade. Density is low
— the lacquer ground carries as much visual weight as the content. The
invariant is bilateral symmetry around a vertical axis; what sits on that axis
and how the geometric frames step outward from it are free.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface is polished lacquer: deep, flawless, and slightly reflective at the
edges. Depth comes from contrast — the metallic ink catching light against the
absorbing dark — not from shadows or layering. The atmosphere is ceremonial and
still, like a great hall before the guests arrive.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks are geometric: thin gilt rules that frame and
divide, stepped borders that recede like a ziggurat, chevrons and sunbursts
used as structural punctuation rather than fill pattern. A single symmetric
device — a centered monogram, a radiating emblem, a stepped arch — often
anchors the composition. Use them to reinforce the monumental geometry, never
as texture or background fill.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is grand, slow, and stately — elements settle into place with the
deliberation of a curtain rising on a great hall, not the flicker of a
machine. Favor long eased entrances along the symmetric axis, geometric reveals
that step outward from the center, and cross-fades between monumental frames.
Interaction, where it exists, is formal and confirmed — a deliberate selection,
a measured response — never casual or instant. Why: the motion must carry the
same engineered dignity as the still composition; anything quick or playful
turns the monument into a toy.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Identity Invariants:
- A deep lacquer ground (oxblood, midnight, jade, or black)
- A single flat metallic second ink (gold, brass, or chrome)
- Symmetric geometry around a vertical axis
- Tall, condensed, high-contrast display capitals
- Thin metallic rules and geometric frames as structure
- Monumental, low-density composition

Freedoms:
- Which lacquer hue and which metal carry the deck
- The specific geometric device (chevron, sunburst, stepped arch) anchoring
  each page
- The scale and placement of content along the central axis
- The exact entrance and transition choreography
- Whether a page carries a single monumental statement or a structured
  symmetric list

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when symmetry breaks: off-center compositions, asymmetric layouts, or
elements that refuse to align to the axis kill the monumental illusion. It also
dies when the palette gets friendly — pastels, soft gradients, or more than one
metallic tone collapse the engineered opulence into kitsch. Dense paragraphs,
utilitarian data tables, and casual illustration all fight the stately register.
If the content wants intimate, human-scale luxury rather than monumental
ceremony, switch to After-Hours Luxe instead of forcing the content onto a
pedestal it was never meant to stand on.
