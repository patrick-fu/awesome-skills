# Magazine Masthead

## Aesthetic Thesis
The screen behaves like a newsstand cover at eye level: a saturated field of
ink-on-paper confidence, one enormous display word demanding attention, framed
by the quiet authority of a masthead that says this has been published, not
assembled. In three seconds you feel the weight of a printed institution —
something with an issue number, a date, and a point of view.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Theatrical, authoritative, unapologetic — closer to a fashion magazine opener
or a quarterly business review cover than to a working document. Reach for it
on deck covers, section thresholds, big statements, and any moment that needs
editorial gravitas: the feeling of being inside a publication, not a
presentation. It fights quiet neutrality and dashboard density; the moment a
slide needs to carry dense evidence or several parallel facts, this register
turns bombastic.

## Color Temperament
Live in a three-character world: a deeply saturated editorial field that reads
like printed ink on heavy stock (rich and cool, never pastel or washed out),
paired with a dark structural ink that carries every rule, border, and primary
text, and a warm paper tone used as an alternate surface to break monotony.
The field is the dominant surface — it should feel like the whole page is
printed on it, not like a background behind content. Accent beyond these three
is refused entirely; the power comes from commitment to a tight editorial
triad, not from variety. Why: a magazine cover earns its authority through
color discipline — introduce a fourth hue and the page stops feeling like a
publication and starts feeling like a UI mockup.

## Typographic Voice
Display type is the protagonist: a high-contrast fashion serif with dramatic
thick-and-thin strokes, run at unapologetic scale and the heaviest available
weight. Theatrical, almost nineteenth-century in its confidence — closer to a
playbill than a corporate headline. Supporting text is a clean, humanist sans
used exclusively for chrome: labels, tags, captions, issue lines, page numbers.
Always uppercase with generous letter-spacing, never crossing into display
territory. The role split is absolute: the serif speaks, the sans whispers
metadata. Why: the tension between the theatrical serif and the crisp sans is
what reads as editorial; a single typographic family would feel either too
corporate or too literary, neither of which is a magazine.

## Spatial Temperament
Density is medium-full and centered: one enormous display headline anchors the
page, with supporting elements arranged with clear breathing room. The
invariant is the ratio of hero to support — the display headline dominates so
completely that everything else reads as footnote. Chrome placement is free;
what must hold is a single gravitational center framed by chrome rather than
competed with.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface is flat, solid, and ink-committed. There is no gradient, no blur,
no glow. Depth comes entirely from color-block inversion — a dark tile on the
saturated field reads as elevated — and from heavy structural rules that
separate sections like paper folds. No shadows, no soft edges, no rounded
corners. The atmosphere is that of a freshly printed magazine still in its
mailer: crisp, deliberate, slightly glossy in confidence.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring maker's mark is the double-rule ornament: a single centered word
bracketed on each side by two stacked horizontal rules, giving a theatrical
playbill frame. Used on the cover and on statement moments, never as
decoration. The second identity mark is the masthead-and-footline furniture —
thin uppercase label rows at top and bottom carrying issue, date, volume, or
section information. These are not optional flourishes; they are what makes
the page read as published rather than presented.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement should feel deliberate and print-settled: a clean fade-in of the
masthead, a confident reveal of the display headline, a steady section
transition. Nothing bounces, nothing springs, nothing draws attention to its
own animation. Interaction, if present, is restrained to a quiet emphasis or a
panel swap. Why: this style borrows its authority from print, where nothing
moves; motion that calls attention to itself contradicts the stillness the
whole aesthetic borrows from.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms

Identity Invariants:
- A saturated editorial color field as the dominant surface, paired with a
  warm paper alternate and a single dark structural ink
- A high-contrast heavy fashion serif for all display moments, run at
  theatrical scale with the heaviest weight
- A clean humanist sans used only for chrome (labels, tags, captions,
  masthead), always uppercase with generous letter-spacing
- The double-rule ornament as the signature framing device
- Masthead and footline furniture on cover and closing moments
- Flat solid surfaces with no shadows, no gradients, no rounded corners

Freedoms:
- Which saturated hue the editorial field lives in (emerald, crimson, indigo
  — the saturation and commitment matter more than the exact family)
- The exact scale of the display headline within the theatrical range
- The number and arrangement of supporting tiles on content slides
- Whether the ornament uses double rules, single rules, or none on a given
  spread
- The specific content of the masthead and footline metadata
- Which slides carry the full masthead treatment versus a leaner content
  layout

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when it gets quiet or crowded: a neutral colorless field, more than
three chromatic families, theatrical-weight display set at body scale, rounded
corners, drop shadows, gradient backgrounds, chrome labels in sentence case,
or a slide packed with six or more competing elements. If the content needs
reading-first evidence, dense comparison, or calm document authority rather
than cover-page spectacle, this is the wrong instrument — switch to the
Research Memo style and let the evidence lead instead of the masthead. If the
content needs pure typographic punch with percussive word impact rather than
editorial cover spectacle, switch to Kinetic Type Punchline. If it needs quiet
narrative reading flow with warm paper feature spread instead of saturated
masthead drama, move to Warm Editorial Feature.
