# Maintainer Issue Brief

## Aesthetic Thesis
A clean, structured issue, pull request, or ticket brief in the spirit of modern
open-source trackers, where a task looks actionable and reviewable at a glance.
The point is that work reads as a well-formed ticket a maintainer would trust.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Actionable, engineering-led, and no-nonsense — the register of a good issue
tracker. Reach for it on engineering tasks, handoff specifications, and action
items meant for developers and reviewers. As a reading-first style it holds real
detail, but it fights emotional storytelling and non-technical audiences; the
tracker frame reassures engineers and alienates a general crowd.

## Color Temperament
Keep a clean, light developer-gray ground with thin, quiet borders, and reserve a
small set of status colors — a merge-ready green, an informational blue — for
badges and states. Color means status, not decoration. Why: the trust of a
tracker comes from a calm neutral surface where a status badge stands out; a
colorful ground would erase the signal that a badge carries.

## Typographic Voice
Pair a sharp, neutral character for headings with a clean monospaced voice for
code, file paths, and checklist items, exactly as a real tracker mixes prose and
code. Keep the monospaced elements crisp and aligned. Why: the credibility rests
on looking like a real engineering artifact; without the mono/prose mix it stops
reading as a ticket.

## Spatial Temperament
It wants a ticket sensibility: a clear title with a status, then well-labeled
sections a reviewer can scan (goal, constraints, verification), or a
before-and-after that reads as a diff. Whether it is a single actionable issue or
a split diff is free; the invariant is a scannable, sectioned, status-bearing
structure that reads as reviewable work.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface feels like a clean tracker UI evoked, not copied: light panels, thin
borders, tidy badges and code blocks. Depth is minimal and tidy — light
separation, no heavy chrome. The atmosphere is practical, orderly, and
trustworthy to an engineer.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks are tracker vocabulary: a status badge, a bold
ticket title, labeled sections, monospaced checklists with checkboxes, and
diff-style before/after. Use them to carry the real task — real goal, real
constraints, real verification. The status-and-sections grammar is the signature;
generic prose with no structure breaks it.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is checklist and review: items check off, a status flips, a diff's
changed lines emphasize. Interaction can expand a section or reveal a check's
detail. Keep motion crisp and functional. Why: the animation mirrors working a
ticket, so decorative motion would undercut the actionable feel.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Keep constant to stay this style: a light developer-gray ground with quiet
borders, a small status-color vocabulary, a sharp heading voice mixed with a
monospaced code voice, a scannable sectioned status-bearing structure, tidy
low-chrome depth, and crisp functional motion. Free to vary on every slide:
issue versus diff layout, which sections and statuses appear, the badge colors,
and how checks are revealed.

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when it stops looking like engineering: a dark dramatic or warm
background, decorative color, an expressive display typeface with no monospaced
elements, unstructured prose with no status or sections, or showy motion. If the
audience is non-technical or the content is a story, the tracker frame alienates
them — use a warmer report or keynote style.
