# Mechanical Scoring Funnel

## Aesthetic Thesis
A colorful, mechanical machine — part pinball table, part multi-stage funnel —
where messy inputs get filtered, categorized, and scored as they drop through
structured lanes. It turns evaluation into something you can watch happen.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Energetic, playful-but-systematic, and evaluative — the register of a game with
rules. Reach for it on categorization, multi-stage filtering, scoring, and
prioritization, where making the sorting feel active beats a static list. It
works live and reads afterward, but it fights quiet reports and reflective
moments; without something being sorted or scored, the machine has nothing to do.

## Color Temperament
Set a deep, dark ground so vivid mechanical accents pop like lit playfield
elements, and use a small set of distinct bright colors to code lanes, categories,
or scores. The colors carry meaning — this lane, that tier — rather than
decorating. Keep the ground dark and the accents saturated but purposeful. Why:
the dark field plus a few coded brights is what makes the sorting readable and
lively; muddy or all-over color would lose both the energy and the meaning.

## Typographic Voice
Pair a bold, confident character for lane and category names with a high-contrast
technical or monospaced voice for scores and counters, so the numbers read like a
scoreboard. Keep score type crisp and prominent. Why: the scoring is the payoff,
so the numeric voice needs scoreboard clarity while the labels stay bold and
legible.

## Spatial Temperament
It wants a sense of motion through structure: inputs entering, passing through
lanes or pins or stages, and landing in scored slots, so the composition reads as
a path with outcomes. Whether it is stacked lanes, a diagonal funnel, or another
machine layout is free; the invariant is that something travels through structure
and gets sorted or scored by the end.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface feels like a lit mechanical machine: dark playfield, glowing lane
edges, colored slots, a sense of physical channels. Depth comes from layered
mechanical parts and glow, not realistic lighting. The atmosphere is kinetic and
a little arcade-adjacent, but organized rather than chaotic.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks are machine parts: lanes or channels, pins or
gates, score markers and multipliers, and slots where inputs land. Use them to
carry the real logic — each lane a real category, each score a real weight. The
sense of a working sorting machine is the signature; static decorative shapes
break it.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is the machine running: an input drops and bounces through the
structure, scores tick up, a slot lights when filled. Snappy, physical, playful
motion is on-brand here. Interaction can drop an input, trigger a lane, or reveal
a score's reason. Why: the animation is the sorting happening, so still or purely
ambient motion would waste the whole metaphor.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Keep constant to stay this style: a dark playfield ground, a few saturated coded
accent colors, a bold label voice with a scoreboard-crisp numeric voice, a
travel-through-structure composition that sorts or scores, mechanical glow depth,
and kinetic machine motion. Free to vary on every slide: the machine form (lanes,
funnel, pinball), which categories and scores appear, the color coding, and how
the run is animated.

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when it goes quiet or flat: a light calm background, muted undifferentiated
color, a delicate typeface, a static list with no travel or scoring, or no motion
at all. If the content is a calm report or a single reflective point, this machine
is the wrong toy — use a report or keynote style.
