# Mid-Century Grove

## Aesthetic Thesis
The screen behaves like a botanical specimen card enlarged to wall size and
framed in warm walnut: a deep cultivated-green ground, cream serif type that
feels hand-set, and a single rust-orange mark of punctuation. The audience
should sense calm craft and organic warmth in about three seconds — an Eames-era
sensibility, not a forest photograph.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Warm, grounded, patient, cultivated — a well-tended garden at golden hour.
Reach for it on brand storytelling, design retrospectives, nature-adjacent product
decks, and cultural narratives where craft matters more than speed. It fights
high-energy launches, tech-forward pitches, and anything that needs to feel
clinical or urgent; the patience that reads as considered here would read as
slow in a hype moment.

## Color Temperament
Live in a warm botanical world: deep forest green or muted sage as the dominant
canvas, warm cream as the primary text value (never pure white — pure white
reads cold and clinical against the green). A single burnt-orange or rust accent
is the punctuation — it touches kickers, em-dashes, inline emphasis, never a
headline and never a background. The green may carry a faint wood-brown warmth
bleeding from one edge, like afternoon light on a workbench. Refuse neon,
cold blues, multiple accents, and any value that vibrates against the green.
Why: the atmosphere is the green-and-cream warmth; introduce a competing color
and the cultivated calm collapses into noise.

## Typographic Voice
Display type is a classical serif with mid-century posture — letterforms that
feel considered and proportionate, carrying quiet authority through shape rather
than weight. Bold is not the point; the serif warmth is. Body text is a calm,
readable companion that sits back. Labels and chrome use a contrasting precise
voice — editorial metadata rather than prose. The hierarchy gap between display
and body should feel generous, almost polite. Why: the serif character is what
makes the green feel like a curated garden rather than a forest floor; a heavy
sans would tip the system toward industrial, and a lightweight display would
dissolve into the field.

## Spatial Temperament
Medium-sparse and breathing. One primary content moment — a headline, a figure,
a quote — with generous green around it. The invariant is the ratio of field to
occupied surface: the green ground should dominate, roughly two-thirds of the
canvas feeling like open garden. Where the focal element sits is free — centered,
anchored left, drifting — but the ground must always win.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface feels like dyed linen or aged cotton paper — warm, slightly textured
by implication, never glossy or screen-flat. Depth comes from a subtle
wood-brown atmospheric warmth rather than from borders or shadows: a low glow
from one corner that suggests afternoon light through a window. Hairline rules
may separate regions but never shout. The air is still and cultivated — a
conservatory at dawn, not a jungle at noon.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks are a rust-colored kicker or em-dash that
punctuates structure, a cream inset panel that breaks the green like a pressed
leaf, a faint watermark glyph anchoring a corner, and thin hairline rules that
frame without competing. Each is used with intent — to orient, to emphasize —
never as decoration.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is slow, patient, and organic. Entrances feel like leaves settling on
still water: long gentle easing, soft fade-ups, quiet reveals with generous
staggered timing between them. Interaction, where present, is barely there — a
soft focus shift, a gentle highlight — never playful or attention-seeking. Why:
the whole system is built on botanical stillness; fast or bouncy motion
contradicts the calm the way wind would disturb a carefully arranged herbarium
sheet.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Identity Invariants:
- Botanical green or sage ground as the dominant canvas
- Warm cream primary text (never pure white)
- One warm wood/rust/burnt-orange accent, punctuation only
- Classical serif display voice with mid-century posture
- Medium-sparse breathing room; green dominates the surface ratio
- Atmospheric wood-warmth depth, never hard shadows or card edges
- Slow, patient, organic motion

Freedoms:
- Exact green hue (deep forest vs. muted sage vs. olive)
- Accent temperature (more rust vs. more burnt-orange vs. copper)
- Serif temperament (more classical vs. more mid-century geometric)
- Focal placement and composition on the canvas
- Whether to use a cream inset panel, a watermark, or neither
- Which content moment leads on each slide
- Entrance timing and stagger pattern
- Deck length and slide count

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when it gets loud, cold, or crowded: a second accent color, pure white
text, cold blue or neon values, a heavy sans display, fast bouncy motion,
multi-column bullet density, hard drop shadows, glossy gradients, or tech-chrome
UI elements. If the content needs to feel more playful, energetic, or
approachable — if "warm and cultivated" reads as too serious or too quiet —
switch to Soft Pastel Friendly instead.
