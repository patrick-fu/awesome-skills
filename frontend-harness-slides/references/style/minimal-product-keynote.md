# Minimal Product Keynote

## Aesthetic Thesis
The screen behaves like a held breath: one idea, enormous, alone in a field of
emptiness, carried with the quiet confidence of something that has nothing to
prove. The audience should grasp it in about three seconds and feel that the
restraint itself is the luxury.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Premium, composed, unhurried — closer to reverence than excitement. Reach for it
on opening theses, product reveals, section thresholds, and single big claims,
where one sentence or one object must land with weight. It fights dense evidence,
multi-part comparison, and anything that needs several facts on screen at once;
the moment a slide has to explain itself in paragraphs, this is the wrong
instrument.

## Color Temperament
Live at the two ends of a nearly colorless value scale: a near-white warmed by a
hair (never pure white — pure white reads cold, clinical, and cheap here) set
against a near-black deep enough to feel like space rather than ink. Mid-tones
are almost absent; the drama is the gap between the extremes. A single saturated
accent is allowed but kept as a rare guest — it earns its power through scarcity,
so it should touch only a small, deliberate fraction of the surface and never
carry body text. Why: with everything else silenced, one small spark reads as an
event; spread it wider and the spell of restraint collapses. Refuse background
patterns, grids, and gradient noise — emptiness is the material, not a lack of
one.

## Typographic Voice
Type is the only actor on this stage, so it must have real presence rather than
neutrality. Favor a display character with quiet authority — letterforms that
feel considered and expensive, whether that authority comes from the elegance of
high-contrast strokes or from the calm certainty of a very clean, heavy
geometric cut; let the deck's own subject decide which temperament fits. Push the
display to a genuinely large scale and open a chasm between it and everything
supporting it — the size difference should feel almost impolite, so hierarchy
hits instantly instead of being politely implied. Supporting text stays small,
calm, and recessive, often reduced to a faint label that only whispers context.
Why: one dominant voice plus near-silence is what makes a single line feel
monumental; a comfortable, even hierarchy makes it feel like an ordinary slide.

## Spatial Temperament
This style wants extreme negative space and a single center of gravity. It reads
as one focal thing — a sentence or an object — floating in a large calm void,
with the emptiness doing as much work as the content. Composition should feel
weighted and intentional rather than filled; where a region is empty, that
emptiness is the design, not a gap to solve. Where the focal element sits —
centered, or drifting off to one side — and how the void is balanced around it
are all free; the invariant is the ratio of silence to signal, not any position.
Why: the power comes from how much the slide refuses to say, so crowding it —
even with "nice" secondary content — is the fastest way to lose the effect.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface feels like a flawless, matte, backlit panel — smooth, deep, and free
of texture. Depth, when present, comes from light rather than from borders or
drop shadows: a soft, almost imperceptible glow or falloff that makes the focal
element feel gently lit within its own space. The atmosphere is still and
gallery-like; nothing hums, flickers, or competes. Why: any visible seam, card
edge, or hard shadow reintroduces "interface," and this style is trying to feel
like a considered object, not a document.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity mark is usually a tiny, muted piece of chrome — a small
spaced-out label or fine marker set far from the focal element — that quietly
signs the slide without stealing from it. It behaves like a maker's mark:
present, precise, deliberately understated. Use it as a faint anchor for
orientation or attribution, never as decoration; its restraint is the point, and
the moment it grows loud it stops being this style.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement should feel slow, deliberate, and weighty, as if the focal element is
settling into place rather than performing. Favor long eased entrances, gentle
drifts, and soft shifts of light over anything quick or bouncy; a single object
easing in and coming to rest carries more authority than several things animating
at once. Interaction, where it exists, is subtle and confident — a quiet
emphasis, a gentle focus shift — never playful or attention-seeking. Why: the
motion must agree with the silence; snappy, energetic movement contradicts the
composure the whole style is built on.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms

Identity Invariants:
- Near-colorless two-extreme value world with warmed rather than pure whites
- A single, scarce accent that touches only a small deliberate fraction of the surface
- One dominant display voice dropping dramatically to recessive support
- Dominant negative space; emptiness is the material, not a lack of one
- Light-based depth with no visible interface chrome (no card edges, no hard shadows)
- Slow, composed motion

Freedoms:
- Exact hues of the value extremes and the accent
- An elegant versus a heavy-geometric display temperament
- Focal placement and composition (centered, drifting, etc.)
- Which idea or object is the hero
- The specific entrance and transition
- Any interaction model, so long as it follows the content

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies the instant the slide gets busy: a second accent color, filled background
texture, several competing elements, a comfortable even hierarchy, hard card
edges or drop shadows, pure clinical white, or fast bouncy motion. If a slide
needs multiple facts, dense data, or a real comparison, do not force this style
onto it — move that content to a density-built style instead of shrinking type
until the emptiness is gone. If the content wants premium material richness with glass layers and depth rather than pure emptiness, switch to Liquid Glass. If it needs dramatic darkness with one lit statement — a spotlight on a quote rather than a field of white — move to Spotlight Quote Poster.
