# Objective Swiss Grid

## Aesthetic Thesis
The screen behaves like a precision instrument: every element locked to a
visible mathematical grid that is worn with pride, not hidden. In about three
seconds the eye reads order — not coldness, but the quiet authority of a system
that has thought everything through and leaves nothing to taste or chance.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Rigorous, objective, unsentimental — the register of a well-engineered
information system rather than a curated object. Reach for it on data-dense
decks, technical roadmaps, comparative analyses, and any presentation where
systematic clarity matters more than emotional pitch. It fights luxury cues,
centered flourishes, and atmosphere-for-atmosphere's-sake; the same grid that
reads as credible here would read as clinical in a product reveal.

## Color Temperament
Live in a strict achromatic world: a clean paper-white ground against ink-black
text, with mid-grays reserved for structural rules and secondary information.
Exactly one saturated signal hue is permitted, and it serves a single function:
wayfinding. It marks what is interactive, what is current, what is the key
datum — never mood, never decoration, never spread across large surfaces.
Refuse gradients, tinted backgrounds, and any second accent. Why: the signal
color derives its entire power from being the only break in an otherwise
colorless system; introduce a second hue and the grid's moral authority — that
every color decision is functional, not aesthetic — collapses.

## Typographic Voice
One neutral grotesque family carries the whole deck, with hierarchy built
exclusively from weight, size, and position on the grid. Display headings carry
quiet authority through scale and weight contrast rather than letterform
personality; body text is honest, unadorned, and hyphenation-respecting. All
type aligns flush-left, ragged-right — centered text is a form of decorative
softening and has no place here. Why: a single utilitarian voice set to a
mathematical scale is what makes the deck feel systematic rather than styled;
introduce a second family or a display character and the grid starts to feel
like a frame for typography rather than an information system.

## Spatial Temperament
Dense-capable and grid-locked. A strict mathematical column grid governs every
placement — its lines visible, not a hidden scaffold. Elements snap to
intersections; gutters are consistent and mathematically derived. The invariant
is the grid's mathematical relationship to the page — column count, gutter
ratio, module scale — not which cells any given content occupies. Within those
cells, content is free to fill or breathe as density demands; the grid absorbs
both a single headline and a table of figures without changing its character.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface is flat, clean paper — no texture, no glow, no simulated depth.
Hierarchy and separation come from grid alignment, weight, and the thin
structural rules that mark column boundaries and section breaks. Shadows and
soft edges are absent; they would imply a material world the grid refuses. The
atmosphere is that of a well-printed technical journal: clear, unemotional,
trustworthy through precision.

## Signature Gestures
The visible grid itself is the signature — thin hairline rules marking columns
and baselines, proudly displayed rather than erased. A flush-left ragged-right
text block set to the mathematical scale, a single signal-color datum marker,
and a photograph treated as objective evidence (cropped to the grid, never
bleeding for drama) are the recurring maker's marks. Every gesture serves
information structure; none exists for its own visual pleasure.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is precise, minimal, and grid-respecting — short mechanical fades or
snaps into position, never eased drifts, never bounces, never anything implying
mass or personality. Interaction feedback is a crisp state change: a signal-color
highlight, a rule weight shift, a cell outline.
Why: motion here must reinforce the system's precision, not perform
for attention; organic or playful movement would contradict the instrumental
character the entire grid establishes.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Identity Invariants:
- Achromatic base (paper-white ground, ink-black text, gray structural rules)
- Exactly one saturated signal hue used only for functional wayfinding
- Single neutral grotesque family; hierarchy from weight, size, position only
- Strict visible mathematical column grid with hairline rules
- Flush-left ragged-right alignment; centered text forbidden
- Flat surface, no shadows, no gradients, no texture

Freedoms:
- Column count and gutter ratio (any mathematically consistent system)
- Which signal hue is chosen (any single saturated color)
- Content density per slide — sparse headline or packed data table, both valid
- Photo selection and crop (as long as treated as evidence, not atmosphere)
- Specific motion timing (within the precise-minimal register)
- Which grid cells any element occupies

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies the moment ornament appears: a second accent color, gradient fills,
centered text, soft shadows, decorative photography, or any typeface with
expressive personality. If the deck needs to feel premium, reverent, or
emotionally pitched rather than systematic and objective, this is the wrong
instrument — switch to Minimal Product Keynote instead of softening the grid
until it loses its authority. If the content is a structured comparison across
options and criteria rather than pure systematic grid layout, switch to
Benchmark Matrix. If it needs calm evidence-led document authority with
editorial hierarchy instead of mathematical rigor, move to Research Memo.
