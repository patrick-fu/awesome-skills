# Operating Manual

## Aesthetic Thesis
A compact, high-contrast industrial runbook or terminal guide that turns a complex
process into a repeatable, high-priority procedure. The point is command: clear,
numbered, do-this-then-that instruction that reads as operationally important.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Commanding, procedural, and no-frills — the register of an operational runbook.
Reach for it on runbooks, repeatable habits, and command-line guides, where a
crisp step-by-step "how-to" is the message. As a reading-first style it carries
real detail, but it fights reflective closings and emotional beats; the industrial
tone drives action and flattens nuance.

## Color Temperament
Set a deep industrial-dark ground and drive it with a high-visibility warning tone
against crisp light text, evoking safety signage and terminals. The palette is
tiny and functional — dark, warning, light. Why: the high-contrast warning look is
what signals operational priority and keeps steps scannable under pressure; softer
or broader color would drain the urgency the style depends on.

## Typographic Voice
Favor a bold, monospaced, terminal character throughout, because the runbook and
command-line feel depend on it and because monospacing keeps steps and output
aligned. Keep it strong and utilitarian. Why: the authority of a manual rests on
looking like a real terminal or ops document; a soft or proportional voice would
undercut the command.

## Spatial Temperament
It wants a procedural sensibility: a strong operational header and a clear,
ordered sequence of direct steps, or a command with annotated output. Whether it
is a numbered runbook or a command console is free; the invariant is a scannable,
ordered, do-this procedure that reads as high-priority.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface feels like an industrial terminal: dark, high-contrast, hard-edged,
with warning-striped or terminal-block chrome. Depth is flat and functional —
emphasis from contrast and striping, not shadow. The atmosphere is urgent,
disciplined, and operational.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks are runbook vocabulary: a bold operational header
(warning-striped feel), numbered direct steps, terminal command blocks, and
annotated output. Use them to carry the real procedure — real steps, real
commands. The high-contrast ordered-procedure grammar is the signature; a soft
decorative layout breaks it.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is step execution: steps advance one at a time, a command runs and its
output reveals, a status confirms completion. Interaction can step through the
runbook or reveal output. Keep motion crisp and sequential. Why: the animation
mirrors executing a procedure, so decorative motion would undercut the
operational discipline.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Keep constant to stay this style: a deep industrial-dark ground driven by a
high-visibility warning tone and crisp light text, a bold monospaced terminal
voice, a scannable ordered procedure, flat high-contrast depth, and crisp
sequential motion. Free to vary on every slide: numbered runbook versus command
console, which steps and commands appear, and how execution is revealed.

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when it softens: a light or warm background, gentle or decorative color, a
proportional or elegant typeface, unordered prose with no steps, or ambient
motion. If the moment is reflective, emotional, or a single big claim, the
industrial command tone is wrong — use a keynote or quote style instead.
