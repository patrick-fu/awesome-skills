# Red-Wedge Agitprop

## Aesthetic Thesis
The screen behaves like a political poster pasted to a brick wall at dawn:
bold, uncompromising, diagonal energy slicing through the field. In three
seconds the viewer should feel the thrust — a red wedge driving into black,
fragments of reality colliding, type standing at attention.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Urgent, confrontational, unapologetic — the register of a manifesto or rally
cry. Reach for it on launches, calls to action, movement-building, and content
that needs to galvanize rather than persuade. It fights nuance, calm premium
reserve, and data-heavy analysis; the same blunt force that reads as conviction
here would read as crass in a research readout or luxury reveal.

## Color Temperament
Live in a triad: raw paper or off-white ground, deep black, and a single
searing red that carries all the emotional charge. The red is never a tint or
a shade — it is full, flat, and unmodulated, used as a shape or a bar rather
than a background wash. A second primary is permitted once, as a rare guest,
but never more. No gradients, no mid-tones, no atmospheric color. Why: the
entire emotional payload rides on the collision of these three; introduce a
fourth value and the poster loses its fist. Refuse pastels, gradients, and any
color that whispers instead of shouts.

## Typographic Voice
Type is architecture, not decoration. Favor a heavy, geometric, unadorned sans
that feels built from steel girders — wide shoulders, blunt terminals, no
calligraphic softness. Set it on diagonals and verticals, stack it into bars
and wedges, let it act as a structural beam holding the composition together.
Bilingual stacking is natural here; two language blocks can brace each other
like crossed timbers. Hierarchy is delivered through scale and angle, not
subtle weight shifts — the dominant line is enormous and tilted, the
supporting line is smaller and locked to a different axis. Why: when type
behaves as a structural element rather than a label, the page gains physical
momentum; delicate typographic hierarchy would dissolve into the noise.

## Spatial Temperament
Dense and charged, with no wasted ground. Every element pushes against its
neighbors — the composition feels like forces in tension rather than objects
in balance. The grid is felt but never visible: diagonals slice across the
field, verticals anchor the edges, fragments of imagery break the plane. The
invariant is the diagonal thrust — at least one dominant vector cutting across
the composition, with all other elements aligning or resisting it. Where that
diagonal originates and how many fragments populate the field are free; what is
not free is a static, centered, symmetrical arrangement.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface is raw paper: slightly textured, uncoated, the color of old
posters or newsprint. Depth comes from overlap and contrast, not shadow or
light. One shape simply sits in front of another; the edge is hard. The
atmosphere is kinetic and dusty, like a street poster pasted over three times.
No gloss, no glow, no digital polish.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks: a red geometric wedge or bar driving into the
composition, photomontage fragments cropped hard and set at angles, heavy type
blocks functioning as structural members, and at least one strong diagonal
that the eye cannot ignore. These are not decorations — each carries rhetorical
force. The wedge divides; the fragment asserts reality; the type bar declares.
Use them with intent; a wedge placed without conviction is just a triangle.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is sharp, angular, insistent. Elements snap into place along diagonal
vectors, slide in from off-screen edges, or assemble like a poster being
pasted up in real time. Easing is abrupt — things arrive and stop, they do not
drift. If a transition rotates, it rotates on a diagonal axis. Interaction,
where present, is decisive: a tap triggers a hard cut, not a gentle reveal.
Why: the motion must match the rhetoric; smooth, eased transitions would
defang the urgency the same way a polite delivery weakens a protest chant.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Identity Invariants:
- Red, black, and raw paper triad — the three-value world is non-negotiable
- At least one dominant diagonal vector driving the composition
- Photomontage fragments or found-image elements, cropped hard
- Heavy geometric sans type used as structural bars or wedges, never floating
- Flat color, no gradients, no soft shadows
- Angular, kinetic motion that arrives on vectors

Freedoms:
- Which diagonal angle and origin, how many diagonals
- Number and subject of photomontage fragments
- Whether a second primary appears, and where
- Exact scale of type blocks and their stacking logic
- Bilingual treatment — stacked, crossed, or sequential
- Slide-to-slide transition rhythm — staccato or building

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when it gets polite: rounded corners, soft gradients, delicate serif
type, centered symmetry, premium gloss, or calm whitespace used as breathing
room. It also breaks under diagonal overload — three or more competing vectors
fighting each other instead of reinforcing a single thrust. Nuanced data
visualization has no place here; the style cannot carry gradations of meaning.
If the content is pure typographic punch without the photomontage or geometric
wedge apparatus, switch to Kinetic Type Punchline instead. If the content calls
for dense bulletin-board boldness with thick borders and hard offset shadows
rather than red-wedge geometric rhetoric, switch to Neo-Brutalist Bulletin.
