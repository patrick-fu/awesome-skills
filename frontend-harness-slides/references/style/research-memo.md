# Research Memo

## Aesthetic Thesis
A clean, modern research brief or executive memo that earns trust through
evidence and clear hierarchy, giving a calm, authoritative reading experience.
The point is credibility: the page should feel like a considered document, not a
slide.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Calm, authoritative, and evidence-first — the register of a well-edited memo.
Reach for it on research findings, executive summaries, and evidence-led readouts
meant to be read without a speaker. As a reading-first style it holds real detail,
but it fights high-energy launches and playful moods; the restraint that reads as
credible here would read as flat in a hype moment.

## Color Temperament
Keep a bright, clean, paper-like ground with crisp thin rules, and hold color
extremely in reserve — a single deep, serious accent for a key metric or heading
is enough. The near-absence of color is the credibility signal. Why: authority
here comes from restraint and hierarchy; more than one accent starts to look like
marketing rather than evidence.

## Typographic Voice
Pair an elegant, considered character for headers and pull quotes with a highly
readable, neutral voice for body text and data, so the page reads like a
well-set document with real hierarchy. Let the header voice carry gravitas and
the body voice carry clarity. Why: the mix of an authoritative display and a
clean body is what makes a dense page feel credible and easy to read; a single
utilitarian voice would feel like a memo template, and an expressive display
would undercut the seriousness.

## Spatial Temperament
It wants a document sensibility: a clear header or masthead, strong hierarchy, and
room for structured findings alongside highlighted evidence, all readable on their
own. Whether it uses a memo header with columns, a centered evidence block, or
another editorial arrangement is free; the invariant is calm, self-contained
reading order with visible hierarchy — never cramped, never shrunk to fit.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface is clean paper: bright, flat, lightly ruled, free of texture or
pattern. Depth is almost nil — hierarchy comes from type scale, weight, and thin
rules, not shadows. The atmosphere is quiet, credible, and unhurried.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks are editorial: a formal header (subject, date,
author feel), thin dividing rules, structured findings, and one highlighted
metric or evidence callout per page. Use them to organize real evidence, not to
decorate. The restrained editorial hierarchy is the signature; loud color or
heavy chrome breaks it.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is minimal and dignified: gentle section fades, a quiet reveal of a key
figure, nothing that competes with reading. Interaction, if any, is subtle —
expanding a note, revealing a source. Keep it calm. Why: this is a document meant
to be read, so lively motion would contradict the authoritative stillness.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms

Identity Invariants:
- Bright clean paper ground with thin rules
- Extremely restrained single serious accent (one accent maximum)
- Elegant header voice paired with a readable body voice
- Self-contained reading hierarchy — never cramped, never shrunk to fit
- Near-flat depth; hierarchy from type scale, weight, and thin rules
- Quiet, dignified motion

Freedoms:
- Editorial layout (memo header with columns, centered evidence block, etc.)
- Which findings and metrics lead
- Exact accent hue within the serious family
- How sections are revealed

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when it gets loud: multiple accent colors, filled backgrounds or texture,
an expressive display typeface, cramped shrunken text, heavy shadows, or animated
flourishes. If the content wants energy, spectacle, or a single big emotional
claim, this is the wrong register — use a keynote or punchline style instead.
If the content needs a formal reasoning chain with context-decision-trade-offs-verification structure rather than a calm evidence brief, switch to Decision Record. If it wants classical scholarly authority with warm serif intimacy instead of modern memo restraint, move to Scholar's Vellum.
