# Riso Print Zine

## Aesthetic Thesis
The screen behaves like a freshly pulled risograph print still warm from the
drum: ink sits thick on toothy paper, color layers drift slightly off-register,
and the whole thing hums with the happy accident of a machine that never prints
the same way twice. In three seconds the eye registers paper grain, a two- or
three-color limit, and the unmistakable wobble of hand-placed layers.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Warm, scrappy, unpolished — the mood of a zine fair table. Reach for it on
manifestos, workshop decks, cultural commentary, and anything that benefits
from a human, slightly rebellious texture. It fights corporate polish and
clinical data; the handmade warmth that reads as authentic here would read as
unprofessional in a boardroom.

## Color Temperament
Live inside a warm paper value: a cream or khaki ground that reads as aged
stock, never pure white. On top sit two or — at most — three spot inks, chosen
for character rather than gamut coverage. One ink carries structure; a second
carries emphasis; a rare third may tag table categories, never as a general
accent. Overprint zones where inks meet are celebrated — that muddy third tone
is part of the charm. Why: the constraint of a few inks is what makes every
color decision feel hand-pressed; a full palette turns this into a cheap poster
instead of a print. Refuse gradients, photography, and pure digital white.

## Typographic Voice
Pair a loud, condensed, all-caps display voice with a quiet, readable body
voice, and throw in a handwritten accent for notes and attributions. The
display voice is dense and poster-like — it shouts the headline, the statistic.
The body voice is small, calm, set tight in columns. The script voice whispers
in the margins: a byline, an annotation, a form-field fill. Hierarchy hits
through the contrast between these three, not through subtle size steps. Why:
the zine register depends on friction between typeset structure and handwritten
warmth; a single comfortable voice would read as a newsletter, not a print
artifact.

## Spatial Temperament
Medium-high density with a deliberate refusal to align perfectly. Elements
cluster and overlap like items on a desk — collage pieces sit at slight angles,
stamps drift off-center, tape crosses borders. The grid exists as a suggestion
the layout keeps disobeying. The invariant is one dominant text moment
supported by a field of smaller marks (labels, stamps, dividers, hand-script);
how they scatter is free, but the ratio of loud to quiet must hold.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface is warm, fibrous paper — you can almost feel the tooth. A fine
grain overlays everything, unifying every flat color block into the printed
register. Depth comes from offset color slabs and slight rotations, not shadows
or blur; a card sits on a slightly larger sheet, shifted, reading as layered
paper rather than elevated UI. The air is lo-fi, earnest, tactile.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring maker's marks: grain overlay on every surface, slight
misregistration between color layers (a headline drifts a hair from its
outline), small rotations on stamps and collage pieces, translucent
masking-tape strips crossing joints, and a hand-script byline beneath every
quote. These are evidence of a hand and a press, not decoration. Use with
intent: a stamp earns its rotation by being a seal, tape earns its angle by
holding something down.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement should feel like print registration shifting, not digital easing.
Favor quick, slightly jerky entrances that settle with a small offset — as if
the paper fed through the drum at an angle. Transitions read as page flips or
ink layers locking into place, never smooth crossfades. Interaction is blunt:
a click feels like a stamp press, a hover like ink spreading. Why: motion must
agree with the lo-fi material; buttery ease contradicts the handmade argument
and exposes the screen beneath.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Identity Invariants:
- Warm paper-grain ground, never pure white or smooth
- Two to three spot inks maximum — never full-color
- Misregistration texture between color layers
- Three-voice typography: condensed display, quiet body, hand-script accent
- Offset color slabs and small rotations for depth, never soft shadows
- Grain overlay on every slide

Freedoms:
- Which specific hues the spot inks land on
- Exact density and arrangement of collage elements
- Single dominant statement vs multi-column spread per slide
- Rotation angles of stamps and collage pieces
- Hand-script content and placement
- Which slides earn a stamp, tape, or drop-cap flourish

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when it gets smooth: a gradient, a soft drop shadow, a rounded corner,
pure white backgrounds, full-color photography, or a buttery ease-in all strip
the printed-paper illusion. Over-polished type, too many colors, or
pixel-perfect alignment kills the handmade warmth. If the content needs more
refinement or editorial polish than a zine table allows, do not sand down the
edges — switch to Warm Editorial Feature instead.
