# Scholar's Vellum

## Aesthetic Thesis
The screen behaves like a candlelit manuscript pinned to a dark library wall:
warm serif letterforms glowing softly against a deep warm-dark ground, one
thought per page, patient and utterly still. The three-second impression is
quiet erudition — a private study after hours, not cold tech.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Scholarly, contemplative, warmly intimate — the register of a well-read essay
rather than a pitch. Reach for it on research essays, reflective decks, and
any content that wants to feel considered rather than sold. It fights
high-energy launches, busy dashboards, and anything that needs to feel fast;
the calm that reads as thoughtful here would read as flat in a hype moment.

## Color Temperament
Live in a warm-dark world: a deep ink-navy ground that leans warm (never the
cold blue-black of a terminal), with type in a warm amber-cream that reads
like candlelight on aged parchment. A single muted jewel accent — a dusty,
desaturated teal — is the only non-amber visible color, and it is scarce:
reserved for small annotation marks, a kicker, a counter, a quote glyph.
Why: warmth is this style's reason for existing; cold dark-tech palettes
feel clinical, and the warm-dark-plus-warm-type combination is what makes
the screen feel inhabited rather than engineered. Refuse neon, pure black,
more than one accent, and any color that shouts.

## Typographic Voice
The display voice is an italic serif with real hand-written warmth —
letterforms that feel personal and considered, as though someone wrote
this, not a system. It carries every headline, every numeral, every
featured moment, always italic; italic is the default, not an emphasis
trick. Body uses a quiet humanist sans that recedes — the substance, not
the voice. A typewriter mono handles chrome labels, counters, and the
signature pin-note. Why: the italic serif is what makes this feel like an
essay rather than a deck; a sans display would strip its scholarly soul.

## Spatial Temperament
Low density and centered. Content occupies a minority of the canvas with
generous dark field above, below, and to the sides. The invariant is the
ratio of wall to content: the dark field is structural, not negative space;
it is the library wall, and the content is what has been pinned to it.
Where exactly the content sits is free; that the void dominates is not.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface is flat and matte parchment-in-the-dark. Depth, where needed,
comes from subtle value shifts within the dark family and from hairline
borders so thin they barely register. No shadows, no gradients, no rounded
corners, no glass or glow. The atmosphere is a quiet library at night:
still, attentive, slightly reverent.

## Signature Gestures
The pin-annotation — a small stack of mono lines in the jewel accent,
sitting in the bottom-left corner of every slide — is the maker's mark. It
functions like a typed label pinned beside a framed document: slide
counter, a short phrase, never loud. Other recurring marks: numbered list
counters in the same mono and jewel accent (never bullet dots), a large
jewel-colored opening quote glyph above centered pull-quotes, and an
italic-to-roman emphasis mechanism where a highlighted word inside an
italic headline switches to upright roman in a brighter amber — a quiet
"this is the line" signal without ever using bold.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Stillness is the default. Slide transitions are instant cuts, not eased
moves. There is no entrance animation. If interaction must exist — a quiet
reveal of a footnote, a gentle focus shift — it is minimal, dignified, and
slow. Why: scholarly calm requires stillness; any noticeable motion would
feel like a page turning in a silent reading room, disruptive rather than
helpful.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Identity Invariants:
- A warm-dark ground (ink-navy, never cold black) as the single surface
- Warm amber-cream italic serif as the display and headline voice
- One muted jewel accent (dusty teal), used only for annotation marks
- Centered content with the dark field dominating the canvas
- Flat depth — no shadows, no gradients, no rounded chrome
- The pin-annotation present on every slide
- Stillness — zero or near-zero motion

Freedoms:
- Exact hue of the warm-dark and warm-type pair
- Which jewel accent (teal, muted emerald, dusty sage) — pick one, keep it
- Content placement within the centered field
- Which idea or quote anchors a given slide
- Whether chrome bars appear on a given layout
- The specific editorial structure of lists and stats

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when it gets cold or loud: cold clinical black grounds, neon or
saturated accent colors, a second accent, busy multi-column density,
rounded card corners, drop shadows, fast bouncy motion, or sans display
type. If the content needs routed technical diagrams, system architecture,
or dense data flows, this is the wrong instrument — switch to Signal Pipeline instead of shrinking serif type until the library calm is gone.
If the content needs evidence-led document authority with memo hierarchy rather
than vellum reverence, switch to Research Memo. If it calls for dense justified
newspaper narrative with nameplate journalism instead of manuscript quiet, move
to Front Page Broadsheet.
