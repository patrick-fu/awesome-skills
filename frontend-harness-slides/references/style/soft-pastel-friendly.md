# Soft Pastel Friendly

## Aesthetic Thesis
The screen behaves like a freshly opened pack of pastel markers on warm
sketch paper — every shape is rounded, every color is soft, and the whole
thing feels made to be held rather than studied from a distance. The three-
second impression is approachable warmth: you want to lean in, not step back.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Friendly, unthreatening, gently nostalgic — the mood of a well-loved notebook
or a casual team check-in. Reach for it on onboarding flows, community decks,
educational walkthroughs, and any presentation where the audience needs to
feel invited rather than impressed. It fights institutional gravitas, high-
stakes drama, and anything that demands austere authority; the moment a slide
needs to feel serious or corporate, this style reads as unserious.

## Color Temperament
Live in a warm bone-and-peach world where the ground itself feels sun-warmed
rather than clinical. The palette is a cluster of low-saturation pastels —
soft lavender, muted mint, blushed pink, pale butter, sky washed thin — all
desaturated enough that they sit next to each other without competing. One accent may step forward a half-step in confidence — still pastel, never
bold — for a tiny marker or highlight, never covering a large surface. Why: the friendliness lives
in the pastel softness; push saturation too high and it tips from inviting
into childish, which narrows who it works for. Refuse pure white grounds,
jet-black ink, and any color that could be described as bold or sharp.

## Typographic Voice
Pair a rounded, open display voice with a friendly humanist body — the display
has the soft geometry of a hand-lettered sign, with rounded terminals,
generous counters, and no sharp angles, while the body stays clean, legible,
and warm, never clinical or compressed. Hierarchy is gentle: the gap between
display and body is noticeable but never shocking; nothing screams. Why: the
type must agree with the pastel softness — a sharp or severe display face
would contradict the whole mood, just as a too-cute body would undermine
readability.

## Spatial Temperament
Medium density with a relaxed, breathing grid. Elements cluster with a relaxed
internal rhythm: padding is generous enough that content never presses against
its container edge, but never so much that a panel reads as hollow. The
invariant is content-to-padding balance — padding must always feel like it
belongs with the content, not like filler — not any specific grid. Where groups form, they do so by proximity and shared padding,
not by invisible alignment rules. Where things sit on the canvas is free; the
feeling of roomy but not empty is what must hold.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface feels like warm, slightly textured paper — not glossy, not
perfectly flat, not digital-crisp. Depth comes from soft, diffused lift
rather than hard shadows or sharp borders: a gentle haze of elevation that
says this sits on top of that without drawing attention to itself. The
air feels like a classroom at recess time — the windows are open, there
is construction paper on the tables, and the light has the soft, diffused
quality of a day that is not quite sunny but not grey either.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring marks are the rounded pill and the squircle — every container,
every chip, every button softens its corners to somewhere between a circle
and a rounded rectangle. Optional hand-drawn motifs (a daisy, a star, a wavy
underline) appear as quiet punctuation in corners and edges, never required,
never loud. The rounded geometry is not decoration — it is the warmth mechanism. A
sharp corner on a pastel container reads as a hospital supply; a soft corner
reads as something you can hand to a child and not worry. That is the entire
emotional argument of the style.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement has a gentle spring to it — things settle into place with a small,
friendly bounce, not a rigid snap. Easing curves feel organic rather than
mechanical; nothing arrives or departs abruptly. Interaction feedback is soft:
a subtle color lightening, a tiny scale breath, never a flash or a jolt. Why:
bouncy-but-gentle motion mirrors the pastel friendliness — a stiff linear
ease would read as cold, and an over-the-top bounce would read as childish.
The motion should feel like a smile, not a dance.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms

Identity Invariants:
- Warm bone or peach ground, never pure white or cool gray
- A low-saturation pastel family with at most one half-step-forward spot accent
- Rounded pill or squircle geometry on every content container — no sharp
  text-holding corners
- Friendly approachable warmth as the dominant emotional register
- Soft diffused depth — no hard offset shadows, no sharp borders as identity
- Gentle spring-bounce motion, never stiff or mechanical

Freedoms:
- Exact pastel hues and their count (a handful of pastels is all valid)
- Whether hand-drawn motifs appear at all, and which ones (daisy, star,
  squiggle)
- Layout composition — centered hero, side-by-side, grid of cards, all fine
- Whether the display voice leans more playful or more calm
- Slide density within the medium range — denser for data, sparser for
  statements
- Specific motion timing and spring character, as long as it stays gentle

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when it gets sharp: hard-edged rectangles, pure white backgrounds,
high-saturation colors, heavy black borders, rigid mechanical motion, or a
severe display face with dramatic thin-thick contrast. It also dies from too
much cuteness — if the hand-drawn motifs become the main event, or if the
pastel palette tips into nursery territory, the style loses its adult-
friendliness. If the content needs organic calm and grounded naturalness
rather than pastel warmth, switch to Mid-Century Grove instead. If the
content wants workshop energy with hand-drawn marks and emoji actors rather
than pastel politeness, switch to Sketch Board Emoji.
