# Solar Biennale Poster

## Aesthetic Thesis
The screen behaves like a folded exhibition poster pulled from a museum
brochure: warm paper flooded with a slow solar glow, one enormous serif
statement sitting still in the light. In three seconds the viewer should feel
they are looking at something printed and considered — a cultural artifact, not
a screen interface.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Contemplative, warm, institutionally confident — the mood of a Dutch editorial
catalogue or Mediterranean biennale announcement. Reach for it on cultural
programme decks, artistic research, manifesto statements, and any content that
benefits from slow, dignified reading rather than rapid scanning. It fights
dashboard density, tech-forward energy, and anything that must feel fast or
urgent; the warmth that reads as authority here would read as sluggish in a
product launch.

## Color Temperament
The world is warm parchment and solar yellow. A single deep indigo ink carries
every line of text and every structural rule. The yellow is not an accent — it
is the atmosphere: a flooded field, a soft radial bloom, or a translucent tile
underprint. A faint warm peach may appear opposite the sun, always subordinate,
never touching text. Why: the yellow field sets the contemplative temperature
of the whole; shrink it to a small accent and the poster feeling collapses into
a generic document with a yellow highlight. Refuse pure white grounds, cool
grays, competing hues, and any text set in yellow.

## Typographic Voice
Three voices, each with a single job. A high-contrast serif with tall ascenders
carries every display moment, numeral, and headline at generous scale set tight
— the statement voice. A clean sans handles body paragraphs and, at heavier
weight with wide tracking and uppercase, the eyebrow labels — the annotation
voice. A tabular mono is reserved for numerical metadata: dates, ledger
figures, page numbers — the data voice. Hierarchy lives in the contrast between
these three, not in color shifts or bolding. Why: locking each role to a
distinct character gives the system its printed-catalogue discipline; a single
mixed-purpose face would dissolve the rigour.

## Spatial Temperament
The canvas wants generous edge breathing room and one dominant focal cluster
per surface — a large serif statement, a jumbo numeral, a flooded yellow panel.
Content sits well inside the margins; only full-bleed color fields and
atmospheric blooms reach the edge. The invariant is the proportion of silence
to signal: one thing said well, not many things said efficiently. Focal
placement and orbital arrangement of supporting elements are free.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface is warm, matte paper — slightly fibrous, never glossy, never
screen-flat. Depth comes entirely from light: soft radial blooms of yellow
suggesting a sun low in a room, fading imperceptibly into the parchment. No shadows, no card edges, no raised surfaces. Structural separation uses only the
thinnest possible ink rule. The air is still and sunlit, like a gallery at noon.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring marks: a soft sun-bloom placed off-center behind the focal
element; a persistent mono page number tucked bottom-right at three-quarter
opacity; a wide-tracked uppercase sans label whispering above a headline; and a
flooded yellow panel covering a column or third of the canvas when the slide
commits to its poster identity. Each gesture serves a structural role —
atmosphere, orientation, context, emphasis — none is decoration.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is slow, atmospheric, barely perceptible. A bloom may drift or
intensify over several seconds; a headline settles with a long, gentle ease;
transitions crossfade softly. Nothing snaps, bounces, or draws attention.
Interaction, where present, is quiet and dignified — a subtle emphasis on
hover, a calm reveal of a footnote. Why: the mood is contemplative stillness;
fast or energetic motion would contradict the sun-lit patience the system is
built to evoke.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms

Identity Invariants:
- Warm parchment ground on every surface; never pure white, never cool gray.
- One solar yellow as atmosphere — bloom, panel, or tile, never a small accent.
- A single deep indigo ink for all text and rules; no second text color.
- Three-voice typography: serif display, sans annotation, mono data.
- Depth from light (radial blooms) and hairline rules only; no shadows or cards.
- Slow, composed motion; nothing faster than a calm crossfade.

Freedoms:
- Exact hue of parchment and yellow within their warm families.
- Placement and size of sun-bloom and any counter-bloom.
- Which typographic voice leads a slide and at what scale.
- Layout arrangement: poster, column-split, ledger grid, or jumbo numeral.
- Whether and where a yellow panel floods the canvas.
- Specific content, data, and imagery carried by the system.

## What Breaks the Spell
It breaks when the screen gets loud or cold: multiple saturated colors
competing, pure white backgrounds, tech-blue accents, drop shadows or card
borders, rounded corners, fast bouncy motion, or dense dashboard grids packed
with data widgets. It also breaks when yellow shrinks to a small highlight rather than carrying
the atmosphere — a yellow accent on parchment is not this style. If the content
needs editorial publication gravitas with more formal typographic restraint
instead of solar warmth, switch to Magazine Masthead.
