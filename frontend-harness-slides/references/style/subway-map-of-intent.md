# Subway Map of Intent

## Aesthetic Thesis
A clean, systematic transit map where several independent lines — streams of
intent, constraints, or data — are shown not as isolated tracks but as routes
that converge at key transfer stations. The point is that separate concerns meet
at deliberate junctions.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Systematic, calm, and structured — the register of good public-information
design. Reach for it on multi-track workflows, converging processes, and
milestone mapping, where showing how parallel tracks meet is the message. It
works live and reads afterward, but it fights a single emotional claim; with only
one track and no convergence, there is no map to draw.

## Color Temperament
Keep a light, clean, neutral ground like public signage, then use a few distinct,
high-legibility line colors to keep each track separate and followable. The colors
are functional route identifiers, not decoration, and each stays consistent for
its line. Why: the whole idea rests on being able to trace each line and see where
they meet; a dark or muddy palette would collapse the tracks into one another.

## Typographic Voice
Favor a highly legible, plain, neutral character — the clarity of transit signage
— for station and line labels, so everything reads instantly at a glance. Keep it
even and unfussy. Why: transit legibility is the point; an expressive or delicate
voice would fight the clean-information feel.

## Spatial Temperament
It wants multiple distinct lines and clear junction points where they meet, so the
composition reads as a routed network with intentional convergence. Whether lines
run parallel with checkpoints, sweep in to a central station, or form another
topology is free; the invariant is several traceable tracks plus deliberate
transfer or convergence points.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface feels like a printed transit map: flat, clean, evenly lit, with
confident lines and clear station marks. Depth is minimal and diagrammatic —
everything on one legible plane. The atmosphere is orderly, public, and
trustworthy.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks are transit vocabulary: colored route lines, station
and transfer markers, and clear junctions where lines meet. Use them to encode
real tracks and real convergence — each line a real stream, each station a real
milestone. The traceable-lines-and-stations grammar is the signature; vague
connectors break it.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is travel along routes: a line draws or a marker advances station to
station, and convergence is shown as tracks arriving at a junction. Interaction
can follow a line or highlight a transfer. Keep motion smooth and continuous
along the tracks. Why: the animation is showing progression and convergence, so
motion off the tracks would break the map's logic.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Keep constant to stay this style: a light neutral signage ground, a few distinct
functional line colors, a plain highly legible voice, a traceable multi-line
network with deliberate junctions, flat diagrammatic depth, and along-the-track
motion. Free to vary on every slide: the topology, how many lines, the line
colors, and how travel and convergence are revealed.

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when the map becomes unreadable: a dark or busy background, colors that
blur the lines together, an expressive typeface, a single track with no
convergence, or motion that leaves the routes. If the content is one claim or a
feeling, there is no network to map — use a keynote or quote style instead. If
the content calls for a dark technical instrument-panel flow with glowing nodes
rather than a light transit map, switch to Signal Pipeline Flow. If it needs
structured comparison across criteria instead of multi-track convergence, move
to Benchmark Matrix.
