# Wabi-Sabi Ceramic

## Aesthetic Thesis
The screen behaves like an unglazed ceramic bowl set on a worn wooden table:
imperfect, warm, and quietly alive — you sense the hand that shaped it before
you read a single word. In about three seconds the viewer should feel they have
stepped into a room where silence is honored, not filled.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Earthy, contemplative, and unhurried — closer to a tea ceremony than a
presentation. Reach for it on reflective openings, philosophical framing,
craft-led storytelling, and any content that benefits from slowness and
humanity. It fights dense data, high-energy reveals, and anything that needs to
land with polish or urgency; the moment a slide must perform rather than
settle, this is the wrong instrument.

## Color Temperament
Live in a muted, earthen value world: an off-white warmed by fiber and age,
never clinical or bright; ash-greys and stone tones that feel weathered rather
than designed. A single faded accent — indigo or matcha, muted and soft — is
allowed as a rare visitor, touching only the smallest deliberate fraction of
the surface. Why: warmth and scarcity are what make the palette feel handmade
and alive; add a second accent or push saturation and it becomes a brand
system, not a bowl. Refuse pure white, pure black, and any color that shouts.

## Typographic Voice
Type should feel written by hand and then set with care — a humanist
disposition with generous breathing room between letters and lines. Display
headings carry quiet warmth rather than sharp authority; they speak at a
conversational volume, never shouting. Body text stays open and easy, never
cramped or dense. Hierarchy is suggested through scale and spacing rather than
weight contrast. Why: the letterforms must agree with the material — a crisp,
high-contrast display would read as cold and manufactured against clay and
washi, breaking the spell of the handmade.

## Spatial Temperament
This style wants charged emptiness — ma — as its primary material. Elements
float asymmetrically, never centered with mechanical precision, never crowded.
A single focal gesture anchors one region while the rest of the surface
breathes. The invariant is the ratio of silence to signal: emptiness must
dominate, and it must feel intentional rather than leftover. Where things sit,
how they drift off-axis, and which corner holds the weight are all free.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface feels like washi paper stretched over a clay slab: fibrous,
slightly uneven, warm to the touch. Depth comes from subtle texture and the
soft fall of natural light, never from hard borders or drop shadows. The
atmosphere is still and meditative — a room with one window, morning light,
and nothing competing for your attention.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring maker's mark is a single ink-brush gesture: one confident,
imperfect stroke used as a divider, an underline, or a quiet punctuation mark.
It appears at most once per slide and never decorates — it punctuates a
thought or marks a threshold. Irregular edges on containers or image masks
reinforce the hand; perfect geometric shapes are avoided.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement should feel like dust settling in sunlight: slow, gentle, almost
imperceptible at first, then still. Favor long, eased entrances and soft
crossfades; nothing snaps, bounces, or demands attention. Interaction, if
present, is patient and quiet — a slow reveal, a gentle drift. Why: the mood
depends on stillness; fast or energetic motion contradicts the contemplative
pace the whole style is built around, and the viewer feels rushed out of the
room.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms

Identity Invariants:
- Earthen muted material palette — off-white washi ground, ash and stone
  mid-tones, never pure white or black
- A single faded accent (indigo or matcha), used sparingly
- Asymmetric composition with charged emptiness (ma) as the dominant space
- Imperfect texture over flat smoothness everywhere
- One ink-brush gesture per slide at most, used with intent
- Humanist, generously-leaded type with warm authority
- Slow, settling motion; stillness as the default state

Freedoms:
- Which faded accent hue (indigo, matcha, or another muted earth-tone)
- Exact focal placement and the direction of asymmetry
- Whether the brush gesture divides, underlines, or punctuates
- Content type — a quote, an image, a single word, a small diagram
- Specific entrance timing and transition character
- How texture reads — fibrous paper, grainy clay, or weathered stone

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies the moment it gets crisp: glossy surfaces, pure white backgrounds,
high-contrast sharp type, perfect geometric symmetry, multiple accent colors,
dense data tables, or fast bouncy motion. If a slide needs to feel premium,
polished, or product-launch ready, this is the wrong register — switch to
Minimal Product Keynote instead of trying to make clay feel like glass. If the
content needs mid-century organic warmth with botanical green ground and rust
accents rather than muted earthen stillness, switch to Mid-Century Grove. If it
calls for friendly pastel approachability with rounded pill geometry instead of
tea-ceremony contemplation, move to Soft Pastel Friendly.
