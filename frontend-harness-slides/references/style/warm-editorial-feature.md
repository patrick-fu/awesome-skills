# Warm Editorial Feature

## Aesthetic Thesis
The screen behaves like a well-turned magazine feature spread opened flat on a
sunlit desk: one idea breathes across a warm paper field, framed by generous
margins and a single quiet accent, so that in about three seconds the eye settles
into reading rather than scanning.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Literary, unhurried, quietly confident — the mood of a long-form essay in a
quarterly journal, not a news digest. Reach for it on narrative-driven decks,
thought-leadership pieces, personal essays, and any content that rewards slow
attention over rapid uptake. It fights dense data, multi-column comparison, and
anything that needs six facts on screen at once; the moment a slide must explain
itself in bullet-point stacks, this is the wrong instrument.

## Color Temperament
Live entirely on a warm cream paper ground — never clinical white, never pure
grey. The cream should read as physical paper aged by a hair, not a screen
surface. A single muted botanical accent is permitted per slide: the feeling of
sage, blush, or lemon, always low-saturation, always one at a time. The accent
touches a small, deliberate fraction of the surface — a rule, a pull-quote
marker, a single card fill — and never carries body text. Why: one accent held
in reserve reads as editorial intention; three accents on one slide reads as a
palette demo, and the literary spell collapses. Refuse neon, dark-tech
near-blacks, and any value colder than a warm stone.

## Typographic Voice
Pair an elegant literary oldstyle serif for all display, headers, pull quotes,
and numerals with a clean, highly readable humanist voice for body text. The
display serif carries the personality — letterforms that feel hand-set and
considered, with a generous italic used for emphasis, kickers, and ornamental
moments. The body voice stays neutral and clear, never competing. Hierarchy is
a chasm: display headlines sit genuinely large and open, while body and
captions recede to a comfortable reading scale. Why: the serif/sans split is
the editorial signal — personality up top, transparency down below — and
collapsing it into one uniform voice turns a feature spread into a memo.

## Spatial Temperament
Macro whitespace and an asymmetric, magazine-feature grid. Content does not
fill the canvas; it occupies a deliberate region — perhaps two-thirds of the
width, or a single column offset — while the rest of the warm paper field
breathes around it. The invariant is the ratio of margin to content: the paper
field must always feel generous, never squeezed. Where the focal block sits,
whether it aligns to a left column or drifts toward center, is free. The rule
is air, not a grid prescription.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface is flat paper: smooth, matte, free of texture or pattern. Depth
comes from hairline rules and the subtle contrast of the paper field against a
slightly lifted content block, never from drop shadows or gradient depth. The
atmosphere is still and sunlit — the quiet of a reading room, not a newsroom.

## Signature Gestures
The recurring identity marks are three. Pull quotes in the display serif at a
scale that demands attention, often offset into the margin or floated wide.
Thin dividing rules — hairline, warm-ink, solid — that section the page with
the lightest possible hand. Generous margins framing every slide like a
well-set page. Use these with intent: a pull quote to anchor a key insight, a
rule to mark a section break, margins to let the eye rest. The moment they
become decorative boilerplate they stop being this style.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is quiet and gentle: soft fades, slow dissolves, content settling
into place with the ease of a page turning. Nothing snaps, nothing bounces.
Interaction, where present, is equally restrained — a subtle emphasis on hover,
a quiet reveal of a footnote. Why: the motion must agree with the stillness of
the reading room; snappy or energetic transitions contradict the unhurried
confidence the whole style is built on.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms

Identity Invariants:
- Warm cream paper ground across every slide, never clinical white
- A literary oldstyle serif carrying all display, headers, and pull quotes
- One muted natural accent per slide, used sparingly and with intent
- Generous whitespace framing content; the paper field is load-bearing
- Flat paper material with hairline rules; no drop shadows, no heavy chrome
- Quiet fade-and-settle motion register

Freedoms:
- Which botanical accent hue leads a given slide
- Asymmetric vs centered focal placement within the margin frame
- Whether a given page leads with a pull quote, a header, or a body column
- The exact scale gap between display and body, so long as hierarchy reads
- Italic ornament density — kickers, markers, numerals can be generous or sparse

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when it gets loud or cramped: multiple accent colors on one slide,
neon or high-saturation fills, dark-tech value worlds, dense multi-column
grids that crowd the margins, drop shadows or heavy card borders that read as
interface rather than paper, fast bouncy motion, or clinical white grounds. If
the content demands very high information density — stacked metrics,
side-by-side comparison tables, evidence-heavy research layouts — do not
shrink type to fit the margins. Switch to Research Memo instead, where density
is native and the credibility register is built for it.
