# Woodblock Floating-World

## Aesthetic Thesis
The screen behaves like a hand-pressed woodblock print laid flat on warm
paper: flat planes of unmodulated color bounded by confident key-lines, one
graded sky bleeding softly at the horizon, and a small red seal stamping the
corner. The three-second impression is stillness and distance — a view of
something observed calmly, not performed.

## Emotional Register & Fit
Serene, contemplative, quietly dramatic — the mood of looking out at a
landscape from a sheltered porch. Reach for it on cultural storytelling,
product narratives with craft heritage, or any deck that benefits from a
human, hand-made calm rather than corporate polish. It fights dense metrics
and high-energy launches; the stillness that reads as elegant here would
read as inert in a hype moment.

## Color Temperament
The world is a warm paper ground carrying a small, disciplined set of inks:
deep indigo blues, a single vermilion used only for the seal and the rarest
accents, earth ochres, and a quiet malachite green. Color sits in flat,
unshaded fields — no modulation inside a shape, no shadows anywhere. One
soft graded sky per view is allowed, used sparingly as the only place value
slips. Why: the woodblock palette derives its power from limitation; every
color is a deliberate carving decision, so adding extra inks dilutes the
hand-made conviction and turns it into decoration. Refuse gradients as
depth cues, neon accents beyond the vermilion, and any color that reads as
screen-glow rather than pigment.

## Typographic Voice
Pair a calligraphic or brushed display note — a single line or title that
feels written, not typeset — with a clean, quiet sans body that recedes
into service. The display voice carries personality and a hint of the
brush; the body voice carries information without attracting attention.
Hierarchy is a gap between the two, not a ladder of intermediate sizes.
Why: the tension between a hand-mark and a neutral reading voice mirrors
the woodblock itself — a carved, expressive edge against a flat printed
field — and that contrast is what keeps the style feeling authored rather
than templated.

## Spatial Temperament
Dramatic asymmetric cropping is the default: a shape pushed off the edge,
a horizon set high or low, generous negative space doing real compositional
work. The grid feels loose and organic rather than mathematically strict;
elements drift into position like boats on water. The invariant is the
relationship of flat keyline planes to each other and to the paper ground
— no perspective, no foreshortening. Where elements sit within that logic
is free.

## Material & Atmosphere
The surface is warm, slightly textured paper — not glossy screen, not pure
white. Depth comes from overlap and key-line confidence, not shadows or
elevation; a shape in front simply sits on top, its edge a clean dark line.
The air is still and slightly misty, like early morning on a bay.

## Signature Gestures
The red seal is the only logo-like mark — a small stamp of vermilion placed
with intent, often in a corner or margin. The single graded sky (bokashi),
dramatic cropping that cuts a shape mid-form, and vertical or asymmetric
title placement are the other maker's marks. Use them with narrative
purpose, not as decoration.

## Motion & Interaction Register
Movement is calm drifting: a slow horizontal glide, a gentle fade-in like
ink settling on paper, a soft reveal from behind a key-line. Nothing snaps,
bounces, or accelerates sharply. Interaction is restrained and respectful
of stillness — a quiet emphasis, a panel sliding aside. Why: the
floating-world mood depends on a sense of time slowed down; motion that
feels urgent or mechanical shatters the contemplative distance the whole
style cultivates.

## Identity Invariants vs Freedoms
Identity Invariants:
- Flat keyline planes with no shadows or perspective
- The limited woodblock palette (indigo, vermilion, ochre, malachite on
  warm paper)
- A red seal stamp as the sole brand mark
- Dramatic asymmetric cropping
- At most one graded sky per view, used sparingly
- The brushed-display-plus-quiet-body typographic pairing
Freedoms:
- Which element is cropped and how
- Placement of the seal and title
- Exact hues within the woodblock family
- Number and arrangement of flat planes
- Whether motion is a drift, a fade, or a slide
- The specific subject or narrative each view carries

## What Breaks the Spell
It dies when it becomes pastiche: a Great-Wave sticker slapped onto a
slide, gradients faking three-dimensional depth, dense data tables crammed
into the paper ground, multiple graded skies diluting the bokashi, shadows
creeping in "just for depth," or a third type voice muddying the pairing.
If the content demands modern organic calm without craft-heritage and
narrative distance, switch to Mid-Century Grove instead of forcing a
woodblock costume onto content that does not need it. If the content is
scientific natural illustration with herbarium plate precision rather than
woodblock narrative craft, switch to Botanical Specimen Plate. If the content needs
quiet organic calm with earthen imperfection and ma emptiness rather than
woodblock's formal craft heritage, switch to Wabi-Sabi Ceramic.
